## Executive summary — investment thesis

**What it is, and what the debate actually is.** Credo makes the silicon that keeps high-speed electrical signals intact over distance: **224G/lane PAM4 SerDes**, sold three ways — embedded in **Active Electrical Cables** (AECs, the "HiWire" purple cables, and the overwhelming majority of revenue), as **optical DSPs and retimers**, and as **licensable SerDes IP and chiplets**. FY2026 revenue was **$1,335.1m, +205.7%**, with **68.0% gross margin**, **$445.0m of GAAP operating income** and **$407m of free cash flow** against **zero debt** and **$1.44bn of cash** `[P]`. Management attributes **over 99%** of the FY26 revenue increase to AEC volume at hyperscale datacenter customers `[P]`. **The debate is neither quality nor solvency — both are demonstrated. It is duration.** Specifically: whether an AEC franchise that exists inside a *physical reach window* survives the closing of that window, and whether the optical business Credo is buying its way into arrives before it matters.

**The three hard facts that anchor the snapshot.**

1. **The business is real, cash-generative, and genuinely diversifying — but concentration is worse than the friendlier of the two disclosed tables suggests.** The 10-K carries **two customer tables on two different bases with inconsistent letters**. On a **contracting-party** basis (Note 3): **Customer A 49%, Customer B 32%** — top two ≈ **81%**, with accounts receivable at **53% / 20% / 19%**. On an **end-customer** basis (Item 1): **D 33%, B 32%, E 19%** = 84% across three. Top 10 ≈ **90%** `[P]`. Customer A is almost certainly a contract manufacturer aggregating end customers D and E (33 + 19 = 52 ≈ 49). Reading only the end-customer table understates single-name credit exposure; reading only Note 3 overstates end-demand concentration. **Both must be reported.**
2. **Growth is program-shaped, and the primary record shows a pull-forward.** On **2026-02-09** Credo pre-announced FQ3 revenue of **$404–408m against prior guidance of $335–345m** — an ~18% beat — and *in the same release* guided FQ4 to "mid-single-digit sequential growth." FQ4 landed **+7.4% QoQ**; FQ1 FY27 is guided to **+6.4% to +8.7% QoQ** `[P]`. One **+51.9%** sequential quarter, bracketed by high-single-digit sequential quarters on both sides, with the deceleration signalled in the same breath as the beat. The **+157% YoY** FQ4 headline is substantially an arithmetic artifact of that spike.
3. **The reach window is arithmetic, not opinion — and it cuts both ways.** Insertion loss scales with frequency, so every SerDes doubling roughly halves copper reach. Passive DAC ran ~3m at 400G and reaches **~1–2m at 224G/1.6T**; AECs ran ~5–7m at 800G and reach **~3m at 224G** `[T/T3]`. That *shrinks* the AEC band in metres — **and simultaneously converts passive-DAC ports into AEC ports faster than it shrinks the band**, which is precisely how Credo tripled revenue through the transition bears said would end it.

**Three reasons to own — each with its counterweight.**

- **The SerDes IP is a genuine, market-tested crown jewel, and it is the thing the AEC franchise is actually built on.** Credo licenses 28G–224G SerDes as standalone **IP and chiplets on TSMC N3**, and third parties pay for it — a market test of the IP that its closest public comparable cannot claim `[T]`. The **Blue Heron** 224G retimer (3nm, 30-tap FFE plus 16 reflection-cancellation taps, UALink/ESUN/Ethernet, Jan 2026) is a substantive quantified engineering claim, not marketing `[T]`. Gross margin of **68.0%** on a product that carries copper, connectors and cable assembly inside COGS is the commercial proof. **Counterweight:** it is best-in-class-*adjacent*, not sole-source. Marvell, Broadcom, Synopsys and Cadence all ship competitive 224G SerDes, and Broadcom plus Marvell together supply the SerDes for **80%+ of custom hyperscaler silicon** `[T3]`. Most of Credo's revenue is not the silicon — it is an *integration layer* (SerDes + firmware + PILOT diagnostics + cable assembly + qualification), whose real switching cost is the qualification track record, and that decays as rivals accumulate field hours.
- **The demand environment is as strong as it has ever been, and every plausible customer just guided capex up.** Amazon raised CY2026 capex to **$220bn** (2026-07-30), Alphabet to **$195–205bn** (2026-07-22) with "significantly" more in 2027, Meta raised its floor to **$130–145bn**, Oracle guided FY2027 gross capex to **up to $95bn** `[T]`. Aggregate hyperscaler capex is ~**$725bn in 2026**, with Evercore and BofA projecting **>$1tn in 2027** `[T]`. **Counterweight, and it is a trap worth naming:** **Microsoft's headline cut from ~$190bn to ~$175bn is an accounting artifact, not a demand signal** — CFO Amy Hood attributed it solely to future datacenter leases moving from finance to operating leases plus a useful-life extension, stating that "outside of this useful life impact, our calendar year 2026 CapEx investment expectations remain unchanged" `[T]`. Anyone scoring that as a guide-down is wrong. The deeper counterweight is that capex is scaling roughly 50% faster than AI revenue, pushing payback further out each quarter `[T]` — and with **beta 3.23**, that repricing transmits to CRDO directly regardless of execution.
- **Valuation has quietly inverted the "most expensive name in the group" framing.** At **37.5× forward earnings**, CRDO is the **third-cheapest** name in its peer set — below Marvell (52.0×), Astera Labs (50.8×), Semtech (41.8×) and Lumentum (40.1×), above only Broadcom (23.4×) and MACOM (32.3×) `[T]`. The 91.9× trailing P/E is an artifact of a small earnings base, not an extreme price. **Counterweight:** cheap-versus-peers is not cheap. Forward P/S of ~17.6× on a $43.0bn cap discounts **NTM revenue of ~$2.45bn**, i.e. **+83% implied growth**, and the PEG of 0.83 back-solves to an assumed **~45% sustained long-term EPS CAGR**. The stock is priced for triple-digit growth to persist through FY27 and 45% compounding beyond it. It is not priced for the deceleration the pull-forward pattern implies.

**What has to be true.** All of: **(a)** hyperscaler AI capex keeps compounding through FY27–28 rather than digesting; **(b)** the AEC reach window keeps converting passive-DAC ports to AEC ports *faster* than 224G shrinks the band and faster than rack architectures go cable-free; **(c)** Credo converts a copper-cable franchise into an optics-and-SerDes franchise before ~2028, delivering the **>$600m of FY27 optical revenue** it has guided — in a market where Marvell holds 60–70% and Broadcom >30% of optical DSP `[T]`; and **(d)** no hyperscaler pairs a cable prime with a rival retimer die and commoditises the 68% gross margin. These are conjunctive. **(c)** is the one management has already voted on with capital, and **(d)** became materially easier for rivals in March 2026.

**Bottom line.** This is a genuinely excellent operating business — tripling revenue at 68% gross margin with real free cash flow, no debt, and a defensible piece of silicon at the centre of it. It is also a business whose largest franchise lives in a window defined by physics, whose growth just showed a visible pull-forward, whose three largest customers are ~84% of revenue with **no minimum purchase commitments**, and whose own management bought a silicon-photonics company for ~$750–770m because they do not believe copper alone is the terminal business. The right question is not "is copper dying" — it is **"does Credo become an optics company fast enough, and is the ~45% implied long-term CAGR the price of finding out."**

## 1. Business model & role in the value chain

Credo sells **signal integrity**. Its core asset is a **224G/lane PAM4 SerDes** — the mixed-signal circuitry that transmits and recovers a high-speed serial signal across a lossy channel — monetised through four routes:

| Product line | What it is | Revenue role |
|---|---|---|
| **AECs (HiWire)** | Copper cable with Credo retimer silicon in the connector housing, extending reach beyond passive DAC | **The franchise.** >99% of the FY26 revenue *increase* `[P]` |
| **Optical DSPs / retimers** | PAM4 DSPs for pluggable optical modules; ZeroFlap transceivers | Guided **>$100m FY27**; challenger share |
| **SerDes IP & chiplets** | 28G–224G IP licensed on TSMC N3, VSR/MR/LR/LR+ reach classes | Small in dollars, large in strategic proof |
| **PCIe/Ethernet retimers (Toucan)** | PCIe Gen6 retimer, PCI-SIG compliant at 32 GT/s | Design wins FY26, **revenue FY27** — new entrant |

**Segment reporting is minimal, and got worse during FY26.** Credo reports **one operating and one reportable segment** (CODM is the CEO, reviewing consolidated financials; segment measure is net income) `[P]`. There is **no AEC / optical DSP / SerDes IP / retimer revenue or margin split, ever.** Worse, the **product-vs-IP-license split that existed through Q2 FY26 was discontinued from Q3 FY26 onward** — Q2 FY26 disclosed product $261.3m / IP license $6.7m, and the Q3 10-Q and FY26 10-K present a single "Revenue" line `[P]`. **FY26 full-year IP-licensing revenue is therefore unknowable**, and the margin-mix effect is no longer verifiable from filings. Management attributes the +3.2pt YoY gross-margin gain solely to "improved economies of scale" with no mix quantification `[P]`.

**Quarterly shape, FY2026** `[P]`:

**Geography flipped hard.** Revenue by shipment destination `[P]`: **US $768.1m (FY26) vs $65.1m (FY25)**; Hong Kong $378.2m vs $243.7m; **Mainland China $80.9m vs $80.1m — flat in dollars, falling from 18.3% to 6.1% of revenue**. North America went from ~15% to ~58% of revenue as US hyperscalers displaced Asian ODM channels. Long-lived assets remain Taiwan-weighted (**$68.1m of $101.6m PP&E**).

**Position in the value chain — what Credo is not.** It is **not** a foundry (TSMC, exclusively, for FY2026 wafer production `[P]`), **not** a cable manufacturer (BizLink builds the AEC assemblies `[P]`), **not** a switch-silicon vendor (Broadcom, NVIDIA), and **not** an optical module maker (Innolight, Eoptolink, Coherent). It sells one high-value component into an assembly someone else manufactures, for an architecture someone else — increasingly NVIDIA — designs. That last dependency is the structural fact §5 turns on.

## 4. Technological moat & competing technologies

**Level 1 — competing approaches to moving bits between AI racks.** Reach collapses roughly by half per SerDes doubling, because insertion loss scales with frequency. This table is the crux of the entire thesis.

| Approach | 400G (56G/lane) | 800G (112G/lane) | **1.6T (224G/lane)** | Power/port | Latency | Trade-off vs Credo's AEC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Passive DAC** | ~3 m | ~2–3 m | **~1–2 m** ⚠️ contested | <0.15 W | ~0 ns | Free and passive — but the band that *becomes* AEC territory each generation |
| **ACC (redriver)** | ~5 m | ~4–5 m | ~2–3 m | ~2–5 W | sub-ns | Cheaper than AEC, no retiming — loses on signal quality at 224G |
| **AEC (retimed) — Credo** | ~7 m | **~5–7 m** (9 m demoed) | **~3 m** | ~10 W per 800G cable | ~50–100 ns | Baseline. Full retiming restores the eye; costs power and latency vs passive |
| **AOC / pluggable optics** | 30–100 m | 30–100 m | 30–100 m | 14–20 W+ per 800G | ~100+ ns | ~4× DAC cost, ~2× AEC power, no reach benefit inside 7 m |
| **LPO / LRO** | — | 800G mainstream | LRO re-inserts TX DSP | ~30% below pluggable | low | Attacks *optical DSP* content, not AECs |
| **CPO** | n/a | switch-package | switch-package | 30–50% below pluggable | low | Serviceability is its weakness: a failed laser is a switch RMA, not a cable swap |
| **Backplane / PCB midplane** | n/a | n/a | in-rack only | ~0 | ~0 | **Removes the cable entirely** — NVIDIA MGX, shipping now |

**Where the industry is converging, by reach tier — this is the answer to "optical vs copper."**

- **Intra-rack, 0–2 m:** passive copper and proprietary copper fabrics. NVIDIA's own architecture is the proof — Vera Rubin NVL72 is **all-copper intra-rack scale-up**, with >5,000 copper NVLink cables in the spine `[T]`. NVIDIA SVP Gilad Shainer, on record: *"everywhere you can use copper, you use copper"* — the reason is power, and it is not close.
- **2–7 m — top-of-rack to NIC, adjacent rack, switch-to-switch: this is the AEC window and it is Credo's franchise.** Passive is out of budget; optics costs ~4× and ~2× the power for zero reach benefit at this distance.
- **7 m to row scale:** pluggable optics and AOCs today, with **LPO/LRO encroaching** and **CPO entering at the switch end**.
- **Row-to-row and building:** optics, unambiguously. Credo does not compete here.

**Does CPO and LPO expand or shrink Credo's addressable content? Mixed — and the honest answer is that they threaten the newer, smaller part of the business and are a near-non-event for the large part.**

- **LPO** strips the DSP entirely, cutting up to ~30% of module power — a direct attack on optical-DSP attach. But at 1.6T, thermal and link-budget constraints pushed the industry toward **LRO, which re-inserts a transmit-side DSP** `[T]`. So the 1.6T generation *halves* rather than zeroes DSP content. **LPO has essentially no bearing on AECs, which are a copper product.**
- **CPO's first attack surface is the spine and switch layer — where Credo has little AEC revenue to lose.** Rubin Ultra NVL576 uses **CPO rack-to-rack while keeping copper inside the rack**; the first platform where CPO plausibly displaces intra-rack copper is Feynman NVL1152, ~late 2028 `[T]`. ⚠️ **The CPO timeline is genuinely disputed**: Dell'Oro calls 2026 "the initial volume ramp" and LightCounting expects high volume in 2027, while SemiAnalysis argues manufacturing cost and serviceability push NVIDIA's intra-rack CPO to 2028–29. **This report does not resolve that dispute and neither should a reader** — see Caveat 6.
- **The under-discussed threat is neither.** It is **NVIDIA's MGX cable-free PCB midplane**, which substitutes PCB trace for copper cable assemblies inside the rack — removing the socket without needing optics at all, and doing it in a product **shipping in 2026**.

**Defensibility assessment.** Genuinely proprietary: the **224G/lane PAM4 SerDes and mixed-signal DSP**, evidenced by third parties paying to license it as IP and chiplets on TSMC N3, and by the Blue Heron equalizer specification. Not proprietary: the cable body, connectors, and assembly (BizLink), the wafer (TSMC), and the packaging/test (Amkor, ASE, KYEC, Sigurd). Credo's defensible content is **the silicon plus the firmware plus the qualification record** — the combination, not the components.

**Verdict: emerging (optionality), not durable — and explicitly time-boxed.** The IP is real and market-tested, which is more than most claimed moats can say. But the franchise it supports lives in a reach band that 224G is narrowing in metres, that MGX is removing in-rack, and that three licensed cable primes may now contest on price. Call it two product generations of visibility.

**Reading it correctly matters, because both the bull and bear spins are available and neither is fully right.** Credo was the **plaintiff** and it **won**: it extracted settlement *and licensing* from the three largest cable manufacturers in the world, which is genuine IP validation and the strongest available third-party evidence that the SerDes patents have teeth. But the settlements convert infringement suits into **licensed-competitor structures** — Amphenol, TE and Molex may now build AECs under licence. **Financial and licensing terms are undisclosed**, so it is not knowable from public sources whether these are royalty-bearing licences (a moat toll, accretive to Credo) or broad cross-licences (which would genuinely open the category). **This report asserts neither.** See Caveat 3.

**Source-reliability tiering.** **Tier 1:** Credo FY2026 10-K, the 2026-06-01 8-K/EX-99.1, the 2026-04-13 8-K, Q1–Q3 FY26 10-Qs, Credo IR settlement releases, NVIDIA newsroom, Astera Labs releases. **Tier 2:** Dell'Oro, LightCounting, 650 Group, SemiAnalysis, IEEE EPS, CNBC, Businesswire. **Tier 3 — not presented as fact:** vendor blogs, generic market-research TAM reports, aggregator relays of concentration figures. **Explicitly rejected:** the "AEC market $20–31bn" report family (scope-contaminated), and a Tier-3 relay of quarterly concentration as "34%/27%/16%" which conflicts with the filed annual figures and was discarded in favour of the 10-K.

**Moat verdict: MODERATE and EMERGING — not wide, not yet proven durable.** Not wide, for four reasons: most of the revenue is an integration layer whose switching cost is a qualification record that decays; the SerDes is best-in-class-adjacent rather than sole-source; the category was legally opened to three cable primes with vastly better cable cost structures in March 2026; and the architecture is set by a customer (NVIDIA) that is actively removing the socket. Not absent, because the IP is real, licensed, and paid for by third parties, and because 68% gross margin on a cable-inclusive COGS is commercial proof that the silicon — not the copper — is what is being bought.

**The single most likely way the moat erodes — and it is not CPO.** It is a **hyperscaler dual-source decision in which a licensed cable prime (Amphenol, TE or Molex) pairs a Marvell or Broadcom retimer die with its own cable BOM and undercuts Credo at 224G**, converting AECs into a spec'd commodity. A ~73–85% share in a hardware category at 68% gross margin is not a natural resting point. **Second-most-likely: NVIDIA architectural exclusion** — MGX's cable-free midplane removing the socket, compounded by absence from the NVLink Fusion ecosystem.

## 6. Secular / thematic positioning

Credo is a **second-derivative** play on AI compute with **first-derivative volatility**. The first derivative is accelerator demand; the second is the interconnect required to lash accelerators together; Credo sells one component into that interconnect. Each remove adds a party whose architecture decision precedes Credo's revenue — and in this case that party is increasingly a single company, NVIDIA, which designs the rack.

The secular case is genuine and quantified: hyperscaler capex of ~**$725bn in 2026**, projected **>$1tn in 2027** `[T]`, with every named customer having guided **up** within the last 60 days. GPU density per rack is rising, and rising density mechanically increases the number of high-speed links that must be retimed.

**But the position within the theme is unusually two-sided, and this is what distinguishes CRDO from a generic AI-infrastructure holding.** The same technology transition that drives the bull case — **224G/lane** — simultaneously: (a) converts passive-DAC sockets into AEC sockets, expanding unit demand; and (b) shrinks the AEC reach band from ~5–7 m to ~3 m, contracting the addressable distance. Whether content per rack rises or falls depends on which effect dominates in a given rack architecture — and NVIDIA's MGX midplane resolves it *against* Credo inside the rack while leaving scale-out intact.

Note also the **beta asymmetry**: at 3.23, CRDO is a levered expression of the AI-infrastructure factor, not a diversifier. The 8/18 session — **−13.03% on zero company news, against a ~5% semi-gauge decline, with an analyst target *raise* the same morning** — is the cleanest available demonstration that this name trades on the factor first and fundamentals second.

## 8. Risks

### The Pre-Mortem — the single most likely path to permanent capital impairment

**Not a demand collapse, and not CPO.** The named mechanism is this: **the FQ3 FY26 spike proves to have been a program pull-forward rather than an inflection; FY27 revenue lands materially below the guided >80% because the optical ramp — ~$600m of the ~$1.07bn of guided incremental revenue — under-delivers in a market where Marvell holds 60–70% and Broadcom >30%; simultaneously a licensed cable prime (Amphenol, TE or Molex) qualifies a competing AEC at one of the three customers that are 84% of revenue, pairing its own cable BOM with a Marvell or Broadcom retimer die; AEC gross margin compresses from 68% toward the 50s as the category commoditises; and a 37.5× forward multiple embedding ~45% long-term EPS CAGR re-rates toward the peer median on a 3.23-beta stock with no short base to cushion it.** The equity does not go to zero — there is $1.4bn of net cash, real FCF and a licensable IP portfolio — but a de-rate from ~17× forward sales to ~8× on halved growth expectations is a >60% drawdown, and the 52-week low of $86.49 (against a $308.67 high) demonstrates the range is available.

The tell that this path is live is **not** the share price. It is that **the +51.9% quarter was bracketed by two high-single-digit sequential guides announced in the same breath**, and that **inventory rose 20.6% QoQ against 7.4% revenue growth** in exactly the quarter growth stalled.

### Named and specific risks

**Customer concentration — quantified, and worse on one basis than the other.** Contracting-party: **Customer A 49%, B 32%** = ~81% top two; AR **53% / 20% / 19%**. End-customer: **D 33%, B 32%, E 19%** = 84%. Top 10 ≈ **90%** `[P]`. **No minimum purchase commitments exist**; substantially all sales are on cancellable POs; RPO is $31.9m. Two architecture decisions by two customers can halve this business.

*The honest counterweight, and it is strong:* concentration genuinely improved. End-Customer D fell **63% → 33%** of revenue while **growing ~60% in dollars** (~$275m → ~$441m), as B scaled from <10% to 32% (~$427m) and E from <10% to 19% (~$254m) `[P]`. That is diversification by *addition*, not by losing the anchor — the cleanest available defeat of the concentration attack. But it leaves the business levered to US hyperscaler AI capex as a **single correlated factor**; all three likely move together.

**Supply concentration is more acute than customer concentration.** **TSMC was the exclusive wafer source in FY2026** `[P]` — process nodes undisclosed. **BizLink is the AEC assembler.** Amkor and ASE package; KYEC and Sigurd test. **No long-term supply contracts** with TSMC or others is a named risk factor `[P]`. Taiwan geopolitical exposure is total on the wafer side, and $68.1m of $101.6m PP&E sits there.

**Architecture risk — the structural one.** NVIDIA MGX's cable-free PCB midplane removes in-rack cable assemblies in a **shipping 2026 product**. Credo is **not** a named NVLink Fusion partner while Astera is. Rubin Ultra NVL576 moves rack-to-rack to CPO.

**Competitive risk newly enlarged.** Amphenol, TE and Molex all hold post-settlement licences. Terms undisclosed.

**Disclosure quality is degrading, and this is a risk in itself.** The product-vs-IP-license split was **discontinued** from Q3 FY26; there has never been an AEC/optical/retimer split; **no backlog figure is disclosed** despite repeated narrative references to "unfulfilled backlog"; and Q4 stand-alone customer concentration is not given. Each removal makes the next quarter harder to audit from outside.

**Inventory** is the auditor's **Critical Audit Matter** — $250.8m, **10.9% of total assets**, E&O write-downs $15.1m `[P]`.

**Dilution.** 34.8m shares (~19% of outstanding) remain available under the 2021 Plan; SBC is 13.7% of revenue; diluted count +3.9% YoY. The $750m ATM is exhausted, so the next equity raise (if any) requires a new authorization.

**Governance gaps.** The FY26 10-K contains **no related-party note** — Item 13 is incorporated by reference to a proxy **not yet filed as of 2026-08-22** (due ~2026-08-30) `[P]`. Insurance disclosure is thin: the only substantive statement is that product-liability coverage "may not be sufficient," with **no D&O, cyber, property or business-interruption limits disclosed**, and cash deposits exceed insured limits.

**Named 10-K risk factors** (verbatim headings, not paraphrase): limited number of customers; **no long-term purchase commitments from customers**; changes in global trade policies including tariffs; order and shipment uncertainties; intense competition; **reliance on a limited number of third parties to manufacture, assemble and test**; product defects and warranty/product-liability claims; **"we generally do not maintain long-term supply contracts with TSMC"**; manufacturing yields; **"ASPs generally decrease over time"**; gross margins may fluctuate; **PFIC risk**; Taiwan geopolitical tension; Hong Kong instability; PRC outbound-investment restrictions; the **PRC Unreliable Entity List** and **State Council Order No. 835**; Cayman shareholder-protection limits; **"our actual operating results may not meet our guidance."**

## 10. Sector bottleneck / structural analysis

**The binding constraint in AI infrastructure is power, and the binding constraint in interconnect is the power cost of moving a bit.** This is the structural fact that makes Credo's business exist at all: optics costs roughly **14–20W+ per 800G port** against **~10W for an AEC** and **<0.15W for passive copper**. NVIDIA's stated architectural policy — *"everywhere you can use copper, you use copper"* — is a power decision, not a cost decision, and it is why copper survives at all inside a rack.

**Credo sits on the right side of that constraint, in a band that is narrowing in metres and widening in ports.** The genuinely interesting structural question is not whether copper dies — it does not, inside 3 metres — but whether the *number of 2–7 metre links per unit of compute* rises or falls as rack architectures evolve. Two forces act in opposite directions:

- **Rising:** GPU density per rack increases the count of high-speed links; each SerDes doubling pushes more links out of passive-DAC budget and into AEC territory. This drove a 206% revenue year.
- **Falling:** NVIDIA MGX's PCB midplane removes the horizontal cable traverse entirely — substituting trace for cable without needing optics — and Rubin Ultra pushes rack-to-rack onto CPO.

**A second, under-appreciated constraint currently favours copper:** LightCounting reports **InP/EML laser chip orders running ~30% above output** `[T]`. If optics physically cannot be built at the required volume, copper ships by default. That is a real, mechanical support for AEC demand that has nothing to do with Credo's competitive position — and it is temporary.

## 15. Investment thesis (synthesis)

- **A genuinely proprietary piece of silicon at the centre of the AI interconnect problem, validated by the market twice over.** The 224G/lane PAM4 SerDes is licensed as standalone IP and chiplets on TSMC N3 — third parties pay for it — and three of the world's largest cable manufacturers (Amphenol, TE, Molex) paid to settle and license it after an ITC complaint. 68% gross margin on cable-inclusive COGS is the commercial proof that customers are buying the silicon, not the copper. — **Counterweight:** it is best-in-class-*adjacent*, not sole-source; Marvell, Broadcom, Synopsys and Cadence all ship competitive 224G SerDes, and most of Credo's revenue is an integration layer whose switching cost is a decaying qualification record.
- **The 224G transition mechanically expands the AEC socket count, and the demand environment behind it is as strong as it has ever been.** Every SerDes doubling pushes links out of passive-DAC budget into AEC territory; hyperscaler capex is ~$725bn in 2026 heading to >$1tn in 2027, with every named customer having guided **up** in the last 60 days. — **Counterweight:** the same transition shrinks the AEC band from ~5–7m to ~3m, and **NVIDIA's MGX cable-free PCB midplane removes in-rack cable assemblies in a product shipping now** — a headwind that needs no optics at all to hurt.
- **Financial quality is unusually high for a hypergrowth semiconductor name.** $464.3m of operating cash flow against $472.3m of GAAP net income (~86% FCF conversion), zero debt, $1.44bn cash, improving DSO, and a gross margin that has been *stable* while the closest comparable's is compressing. — **Counterweight:** $31.9m of RPO against a $1.34bn run-rate, no minimum customer commitments, but **$359.9m of Credo's own non-cancellable purchase obligations** — a demand/supply asymmetry that converts any air-pocket into shortfall fees and excess inventory, with inventory already up 20.6% QoQ on 7.4% revenue growth.
- **Concentration is genuinely improving, by addition rather than subtraction.** The top end-customer fell 63% → 33% of revenue *while growing ~60% in dollars*, as two new customers scaled to ~$427m and ~$254m. — **Counterweight:** top three are still 84%, top ten ~90%, and on a contracting-party basis one intermediary is **49% of revenue and 53% of receivables**. All three are levered to the same US hyperscaler AI-capex factor, so they likely move together.

**What has to be true:** hyperscaler AI capex keeps compounding **and** 224G converts DAC ports to AEC ports faster than MGX and CPO remove them **and** the guided >$600m of FY27 optical revenue arrives in Marvell and Broadcom's home market **and** no licensed cable prime commoditises the 68% margin. Conjunctive — and the optical clause is the one with the least evidence and the most capital already committed to it.

## 18. Final thoughts & conclusion

Credo is the best operating business this framework has looked at in some time: revenue tripled to $1.34bn, gross margin 68% and stable, $407m of free cash flow, zero debt, $1.44bn of cash, and a piece of silicon so defensible that the three largest cable manufacturers on earth paid to license it rather than litigate. Every thread in this report confirms that. **And every thread also converges on a single unresolved question, which is not whether the business is good but how long the largest part of it lasts.**

The **business model** sells one component into an assembly someone else builds for an architecture NVIDIA designs. The **catalysts** are dominated by one date ten days out. The **moat** is real silicon wrapped in a decaying qualification advantage, in a category legally opened to three cable primes in March 2026. The **financials** show genuine cash conversion alongside a demand/supply asymmetry — $31.9m of cancellable customer backlog against $359.9m of Credo's own non-cancellable obligations — and inventory building faster than revenue. The **valuation** is the third-cheapest forward P/E in its peer group while embedding ~83% NTM revenue growth and ~45% long-term EPS CAGR. And the **sentiment** data shows a 3.23-beta stock that fell 13% in a session on no news whatsoever, with options at the 1.6th percentile of implied vol going into a print.

### Pre-investment checklist

- **Your edge — what do you believe that the crowd doesn't?** The crowd debates **"copper versus optics"** and treats it as a binary that Credo either wins or loses. That framing is wrong in both directions, and the sell-side's 56%-of-average target dispersion is evidence nobody has resolved it. The variant view is that **the copper-versus-optics question is already settled and is not the risk**: copper wins inside ~3 metres on power, permanently, and NVIDIA says so explicitly — while optics wins beyond ~7 metres, equally permanently. Credo's franchise does not live or die on that boundary moving. **It lives or dies on how many 2-to-7-metre links exist per unit of compute — and that is an architecture decision made by NVIDIA, not a physics outcome.** MGX's cable-free PCB midplane removes AEC sockets *without any optics involved at all*, which means the most-discussed threat (CPO) is not the operative one and the operative one (rack topology) is barely discussed. The corollary is that the FY27 optical guide is not a hedge against CPO — it is a hedge against **Credo's own sockets being designed out of the rack**, which is why management spent ~$750–770m on it. **If you do not hold a specific view on rack topology two generations out, you do not have an edge here; you are underwriting a physics debate that was already decided and missing the architecture one that wasn't.**
- **Your tripwire — what would prove you wrong?** Pre-commit to re-underwriting or exiting if ANY of these fire: **(1)** the **2026-09-01 Q1 FY27 print** lands below the $465m guide floor, **or** management withdraws, lowers, or fails to reaffirm the **>80% FY27 growth** guide, **or** declines to give a 2H shape that makes it arithmetic — confirming the FQ3 spike was a pull-forward; **(2)** a **licensed cable prime (Amphenol, TE Connectivity or Molex) or Astera Labs is disclosed or credibly reported to have won a dual-source or design-win at any customer representing ≥19% of Credo revenue** — the commoditisation mechanism in the Pre-Mortem; **(3)** **GAAP gross margin prints below 65%** in any quarter, or management guides below 65%, signalling AEC price competition or an adverse optical mix; **(4)** **FY2027 optical revenue tracks below ~$400m** on a run-rate basis by the FQ3 FY27 print (~March 2027), i.e. the >$600m guide misses by a third or more — the optical conversion failing; **(5)** **NVIDIA discloses a rack architecture that eliminates third-party AECs from the scale-out tier**, or Credo is publicly excluded from a major NVIDIA interconnect ecosystem while a named rival is included.
- **Time-horizon fit.** The *tripwires* resolve fast — #1 in ten days, #3 quarterly, #2 and #5 on announcement. The *thesis* needs three to five years and an optical business that is under one quarter old. That mismatch is the core risk: a multi-year architectural bet held in a security with 3.23 beta and 105% realized volatility.
- **Conviction-appropriate sizing.** **Event-driven in the near term, architecturally binary in the long term.** The 2026-09-01 print is a genuine coin-flip on a stock that has moved 13% in a session on nothing. Size to survive that print being wrong, not to be right about it — and note that the floor ($1.44bn net cash, positive earnings, real FCF) is genuinely supported, so this is de-rate risk rather than impairment risk.
- **What kills the thesis fastest.** A Q1 FY27 miss or a soft FY27 reaffirmation on 2026-09-01 — faster-acting than any architectural or competitive outcome, and the specific mechanism in the §8 Pre-Mortem. It is also the most likely, because the sequential-growth pattern that suggests it is already in the public record.
- **Where the price sits relative to the thesis.** **Cheap against peers, priced for the tailwind and not the headwind.** 37.5× forward is third-lowest in the group; ~+83% implied NTM revenue growth and ~45% implied long-term EPS CAGR leave no room for the deceleration the FQ4/FQ1 guides imply. At 25.3% below the 52-week high the entry is better than it has been in months — conditional entirely on the print.
- **What to monitor next.** **2026-09-01 Q1 FY27 results and the FY27 2H shape** · the **DEF 14A** (due ~2026-08-30 — closes the related-party and compensation gaps) · **optical revenue disclosure** and whether Credo restores any product-line split · **gross margin** against the 65% line · **inventory and DIO** direction · **any TE/Molex/Amphenol AEC qualification** at a hyperscaler · **NVIDIA MGX adoption breadth and NVLink Fusion partner list** · **Astera's Q3 print** and whether it overtakes Credo in absolute revenue · and the **insider-selling cadence** after the print.

**Bottom line.** An excellent business, honestly cheap against its peer group, attached to a franchise whose socket count is decided by a company that does not pay it. The physics question everyone asks — copper or optics — is already answered and is not the risk. The architecture question almost nobody asks is. Management has already voted with ~$750–770m of shareholder capital that copper alone is not the terminal business, and they are probably right; the open question is whether the optics arrive before the sockets leave. **2026-09-01 tells you nothing about that — but it tells you whether the last four quarters were a trend or a spike, and at 37.5× forward on 3.23 beta, that is the question that gets priced first.**

### Numbered key caveats

1. **Fully diluted share count is not current.** 186.48m is basic/outstanding at 2026-06-08; FY26 weighted diluted was 188.2m and FQ3 alone 192.0m. A current figure requires the Q1 FY27 10-Q (~2026-09-01). All per-share multiples carry this imprecision.
2. **The DustPhotonics consideration is unreconciled between two primary sources.** The 2026-04-13 8-K states $750,000,000 cash + ~0.92m shares (+ up to 3.21m contingent); the FY26 10-K subsequent-events note states "$770 million cash and approximately 0.8 million shares." Both are primary; the filings do not reconcile them. This report uses "~$750–770m" throughout rather than picking one.
3. **The settlement terms with Amphenol, TE and Molex are undisclosed.** Whether these are royalty-bearing licences (accretive, a moat toll) or broad cross-licences (category-opening) is **not knowable from public sources**, and the two readings have opposite implications for the moat verdict. This is the highest-value unresolved item in the report.
4. **Credo's NVLink Fusion status could not be established.** Credo does not appear on NVIDIA's named partner list (MediaTek, Marvell, Alchip, Astera Labs, Synopsys, Cadence) while Astera does. This is absence of evidence, not evidence of exclusion, and it sits against Tier-3 reporting that Credo has demonstrated 1.6T AECs into Vera Rubin NVL144 and Kyber Ultra NVL576. Conspicuous but unresolved.
5. **No FY2026 proxy had been filed as of 2026-08-22**, so the 10-K's Item 13 (related-party transactions) and all executive-compensation detail are incorporated by reference to a document that does not yet exist publicly. Due ~2026-08-30. Management incentive structure is therefore **unassessable** in §13.
6. **The CPO timeline is genuinely disputed and this report does not resolve it.** Dell'Oro calls 2026 "the initial volume ramp"; LightCounting expects high volume in 2027; SemiAnalysis argues manufacturing cost and serviceability push NVIDIA's intra-rack CPO to 2028–29. Both positions are presented; neither is adopted.
7. **No reliable public AEC-only TAM exists** with a stated methodology. The ~$4bn-by-2028 figure is a Tier-3 relay of a paywalled 650 Group report. Generic multi-tens-of-billions "AEC market" reports are scope-contaminated and were rejected.
8. **AEC market share is two conflicting single-sourced numbers** — 650 Group ~73% (Q2 2025) and CNBC 88% (Oct 2025), methodologies undisclosed, mutually inconsistent. A tier ("clear leader, ~70–85%") is used rather than a point estimate.
9. **Reach figures at 224G are Tier-3/Tier-4 derived, not measured.** Passive DAC at 1.6T is variously given as 1m, 1–2m and "below 3m"; the AEC ~3m figure derives from SemiAnalysis commentary. These should be pressure-tested against an IEEE 802.3dj channel budget before being relied on quantitatively. The *direction* (reach halves per SerDes doubling) is not in dispute.
10. **Strike-level options open interest was not walked**, so max-pain and put-skew term structure are uncharacterised despite an earnings print ten days out. Numeric correlation/beta to SOX, NVDA, ALAB and AVGO were not computed; the published 5-year beta of 3.23 and the 8/18 co-movement stand in.
11. **The historical forward-multiple percentile band is approximate**, sourced from secondary analysis rather than a terminal-grade time series.
12. **No product-line revenue split has ever been disclosed**, and the product-vs-IP-license split was discontinued from Q3 FY26. FY26 IP-licensing revenue is unknowable, AEC ASP erosion cannot be isolated from mix, and the stated cause of gross-margin expansion cannot be verified.
