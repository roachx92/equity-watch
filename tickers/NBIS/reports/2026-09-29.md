# NBIS — Nebius Group N.V.

NASDAQ:NBIS · ordinary Class A shares (no ADR) · Schiphol, Netherlands · FY ends December 31

*A hypergrowth AI cloud whose two anchor tenants are AAA and Aa3 — financing a capital base that has barely begun to depreciate.*

## Executive summary — investment thesis

Nebius is a full-stack AI **neocloud** — it buys NVIDIA systems, secures power and shell, and sells AI training and inference capacity with its own orchestration and inference software on top, across Finland, Estonia, the UK, France, Iceland, Israel and a fast-growing US footprint. **The debate is not quality and it is not demand; it is price against an unproven return on capital.** Q2 2026 revenue was **$582.3M, +454% YoY** — more in one quarter than all of FY2025 — but the company spent **$8,130.3M of capex in one half-year** against **$981.3M of revenue**, on an asset base whose depreciation has barely started: **H1 D&A of $471.7M is 46% of the ~$1,026M straight-line charge** implied by gross PP&E going **$6,190.3M → ~$14,320M** at the company's own five-year life **[P]**.

**Three reasons to own, each with its counterweight:**

**(1)** The **best counterparty book in the cohort — Microsoft (AAA, up to ~$17.4–19.4B, all tranches delivered) and Meta (Aa3, $12B firm)** — converts into the cheapest capital in it, via a **~$775M facility at SOFR+250 collateralised by the contracted cash flows of "an investment-grade counterparty"** and framed as repeatable against ">$40B of commitments" **[P]**; *but* concentration is near-total and partly undiligenceable — **top three = 59% of Q2 revenue**, none ever named, the customer letters **inconsistent between the 20-F and the Q2 6-K**, and **Customer E holds 29% of net AR on 14% of revenue** with no investment-grade description anywhere.

**(2)** The backlog is **filed and audited rather than marketed** — an ASC 606 RPO of **$21,333M audited**, rising to **$37,490.6M** unaudited, roughly **12.5x** annualised revenue beside CoreWeave's ~10x, against **Nscale's ~368x** (~$103.4B of TCV on $140.6M of H1 revenue, only $2.6B active) **[P]**; *but* the audited figure updates only **annually** (no check until ~April 2027), **$15B of the "$27B from Meta" is an unsold-capacity backstop rather than demand**, and **ARR is one month annualised**.

**(3)** Pricing power is **demonstrated, not asserted** — a reported **~+20% increase effective 2026-10-01**, the second in months, produced the largest company-specific session in the sample (**+4.12% on a day CoreWeave fell 4.25%**, a **+7.62% residual, z = +1.8**), corroborated by **$20–25M/MW ACV, ~70% of deals prepaid covering 50–60% of capex, ~22-month payback** **[P/T]**; *but* that is **scarcity rent** which management itself says may normalise in **18–24 months**, while **~$8.8B of private neocloud capital closed in under a month** (Nscale $3.36B, Crusoe $3.9B, Fluidstack $1.5B) to build competing supply against the same roadmap **[T]**.

**What has to be true — all four.** Asset turn improves **~3.3x**, from FY2025's 0.146x toward the ~0.49x GAAP breakeven requires. Microsoft and Meta keep taking delivery, because **both contracts carry tranche-termination rights** and Meta's $12B block lands in **H1 2027 on Vera Rubin hardware Nebius has only just sampled**. Capital markets stay open at rates preserving the spread on a **~$250B pipeline that is ~20% funded** **[T]**. And scarcity pricing survives three funded competitors arriving.

**Bottom line.** The highest-quality counterparty book in the neocloud cohort attached to the least-proven return on capital in it. At roughly **20–23x FY2026E revenue** ($68.1B EV against $3.0–3.4B guided) the equity is priced for the capital base to start earning — and **the depreciation that will test that has not arrived yet.** The next four quarters are when it does.

## 4. Technological moat & competing approaches

| Approach | Champions | Trade-off vs Nebius |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyperscaler AI cloud** | AWS, Azure, GCP, OCI | Hyperscaler H100 median ≈**$7.89/GPU-hr vs ≈$4.17** on dedicated clouds **[T3]**; near-infinite IG capital. NBIS wins on price and time-to-capacity, loses on bundling and balance sheet. **Note the inversion: Microsoft is a *customer*** — hyperscalers rent neocloud capacity to bridge their own shortfalls |
| **Specialist neoclouds** | CoreWeave, Nscale, Crusoe, Lambda, Together, Fluidstack | Near-identical product; competition on **allocation priority, time-to-energisation, price** |
| **Lab self-build / direct contracting** | OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, xAI | **The existential substitute.** Anthropic–TeraWulf ~$19B/20-yr; Anthropic–Riot $9.1B/191 MW **[T]**. Removes the neocloud margin layer entirely |
| **Rent powered shell, own the GPUs** | TeraWulf, Cipher, Hut 8, IREN | **A substitute for Nebius's capex, not its revenue.** Nebius's own July 2026 asset-light "infrastructure partnerships" move toward this structure |
| GPU marketplaces | Vast.ai, RunPod, **NVIDIA DGX Cloud Lepton** | No SLA or multi-node fabric. **Lepton matters strategically: NVIDIA aggregates its own cloud partners, disintermediating their demand generation** **[P]** |
| **Non-NVIDIA silicon** | Google/Broadcom TPU, Trainium, Instinct | Substitute for the whole NVIDIA-centric stack. Anthropic: up to **1M TPUs / >1 GW in 2026** **[P]**; OpenAI–AWS 2 GW Trainium; OpenAI 6 GW AMD **[T]** |

**The industry is converging on a capital structure, not a technology** — contracted, take-or-pay, IG-counterparty, prepayment-funded capacity, because that is the only structure debt markets will finance at this scale. Nebius has already made that turn (~70% of Q2 deals prepaid, **>$9B of 2026 prepayments guided** **[P]**). **The corollary: the differentiator is converging on balance-sheet access and time-to-energised-megawatt, not software.**

**Defensibility.** *Proprietary technology: largely absent.* **Soperator, the orchestration layer, is open-sourced by Nebius itself**, and **AI Studio ships an OpenAI-compatible API** — an explicit *reduction* of switching cost, not an increase. *Switching costs: unproven, and unfalsifiable as disclosed* — **neither Nebius, CoreWeave nor Nscale discloses net revenue retention, gross churn or net churn** **[P, by absence]**. Every "sticky workload" claim traces to equity commentary, never a disclosed number; a moat that cannot be measured from outside should not be underwritten as one. *Capital barrier: real, and the most durable component* — interconnection queues, permits and 15–20 year power contracts are genuinely scarce and cannot be software-cloned.

**Verdict: technological moat ABSENT. The defensible assets are power, prepayment-funded capital, and time-to-energised-megawatt.**

## 6. Secular / thematic positioning

Nebius is a **first-derivative** beneficiary of AI infrastructure spending on revenue and a **second-derivative** beneficiary on returns.

Hyperscaler 2026 capex guidance is ~**$720–745B** and was raised again in the Q2 round, with 2027 consensus materially higher **[T]**. Nebius sits directly in that path, with the unusual distinction of selling *to* two of the hyperscalers. But the *returns* depend on asset turn, contract duration and pricing — none of which move with the theme. §7 shows EBITDA per dollar of gross PP&E running at ~30% of the depreciation per dollar. **Exposure to the theme is not capture of the theme.**

A distinctive third exposure: Nebius is the only listed pure-play neocloud with a **European anchor** at a moment when EU AI regulation and sovereign-AI procurement are live, and the Palantir partnership is a credible go-to-market for exactly that. But it is **not** CLOUD-Act-clean the way a purely European operator is — Dutch-domiciled, US-listed, NVIDIA on the register, US-dominant pipeline. **Sovereignty is a sales angle that has not yet produced a disclosed sovereign contract.**

## 7. Financials — contracted vs recognized, unit economics, capital stack

### Why a 41% EBITDA margin coexists with a $190M loss

Three distortions, each disclosed but none prominent:

- **"Adjusted EBITDA" excludes both D&A and SBC.** With SBC at **~49% of the figure** and D&A at 46% of its eventual rate, **the 41% margin is not a return on capital and must not be read as one.**
- **Reported H1 net income of +$430.8M is a $780.6M non-cash ClickHouse markup**, not operations. The company's own **adjusted net loss of −$133.5M** is the honest line.
- **The useful-life extension.** Verbatim from the Q1 2026 6-K: *"Starting the first quarter of 2026, we revised the useful life for our server and network equipment from four years to five years… applied prospectively"* — **with no quantified effect disclosed.** A 4→5 year change cuts the annual charge ~20%: roughly **$220–260M/year**, larger than the entire Q2 net loss. *Two honest qualifications:* Nebius at five years is **less** aggressive than CoreWeave at six, so it is not the cohort outlier; and **adjusted EBITDA is unaffected**, since it excludes D&A. What survives is direction and timing — Nebius **extended** asset life in the year the Blackwell→Rubin cadence compressed, while **Amazon shortened** a subset of server lives citing AI-driven obsolescence.

### The depreciation arithmetic — the report's central finding

| | |

| Gross PP&E, 2025-12-31 | **$6,190.3M** **[P, 20-F XBRL]** |

**More than half the asset base is not yet in service.** That is not an accounting irregularity; it is a fact about a company mid-build. But it runs forward, and the forward arithmetic is the thesis:

- FY2025 asset turn: **$529.8M ÷ average gross PP&E $3,637M = 0.146x.** At a 41% margin that is **$0.060 of EBITDA per $1 of gross PP&E against a $0.200 depreciation charge — EBITDA is 30% of depreciation.**
- GAAP breakeven at that margin requires asset turn of **~0.49x — a 3.3x improvement**, i.e. hyperscaler-class utilisation at neocloud-class margins.
- At Q2's **$5.7B/quarter** capex, gross PP&E passes **$25B**. Fully in service at five years that is **~$5B/year of depreciation against $3.0B of ARR** — depreciation would exceed revenue outright.

**This does not mean the business cannot work.** It means the equity case rests on asset turn improving ~3.3x as capacity fills, and **the metric the bull case quotes is structurally incapable of showing whether that is happening.** Watch asset turn and connected megawatts, not the margin.

### Cash flow — positive, and not from earnings

H1 **operating cash flow +$4,504.1M** against **capex $(8,130.3)M** and net investing outflow $(8,288.8)M, with deferred revenue going **$1,577.5M → $5,975.2M** and management guiding **">$9 billion in customer prepayments in 2026."**

**The OCF is overwhelmingly prepayment.** Prepayments are cash-positive on receipt and **cash-neutral on recognition** — so OCF looks hyperscaler-like while the prepayment book is *growing*, and converges toward EBITDA the moment it stops. **Any deceleration in new contract signing appears in OCF before it appears in revenue.** That inverts the usual order and is the single most useful early-warning metric in the filings.

## 8. Risks

### Pre-mortem — the most likely mechanism of permanent impairment

**Not insolvency, and not an AI-demand collapse.** The realistic catastrophic path is a **capital-return failure that becomes visible only as the asset base enters service, compounded by a financing window closing on the way.**

Through 2027 the roughly $14B of gross PP&E already bought — plus whatever $5.7B/quarter adds — **moves into service, and depreciation roughly doubles from its 46%-of-straight-line level.** Simultaneously the scarcity pricing that produced $20–25M/MW ACV normalises, on management's own 18–24 month estimate, as Nscale, Crusoe, Lambda and Fluidstack deploy the ~$8.8B they raised in a single month. **Asset turn improves — but to 0.25x or 0.30x, not the ~0.49x GAAP breakeven requires.** The company is then structurally loss-making on a larger base with a fuller charge.

The prepayment engine then reverses. Deferred revenue stops growing, so **operating cash flow converges from +$4.5B toward EBITDA**, just as capex commitments and **$12.05B of uncommenced lease obligations** begin running. Nebius must fund the gap in capital markets — and the **4.50% coupon on eight-year paper** already tells you what that costs, with the 30-year at its highest since 2002 and AI-infrastructure spreads widening. **Unrated, it has no ratings floor to defend; it has only its customers' ratings, and those are pledged as collateral on the secured facility.**

**The killing detail is contractual rather than financial.** Both anchor contracts carry **tranche-termination rights for delivery delay and availability failure**, and Meta's $12B firm block deploys in **H1 2027 on Vera Rubin hardware Nebius has only just sampled**. A deployment slip does not produce a negotiation — it **arms a clause**, at the exact moment the company needs the contracted cash flows to support its secured borrowing. And the $15B Meta backstop **only pays if Nebius fails to sell the capacity**, which is the scenario where it is worth least to equity.

The equity does not go to zero: Microsoft and Meta keep paying on delivered capacity, the assets are real and the power is scarce. It **de-rates from ~20x forward revenue to a mid-single-digit multiple on a business that grows but does not earn** — roughly **−60–75%** from current levels — and it is permanent because the capital will already have been spent.

**The tell that this is beginning: operating cash flow decelerating while revenue still accelerates.** That divergence precedes every other symptom.

### Named risks

**Concentration.** Top three = **59% of Q2 revenue** (24/21/14); the two disclosed contracts ≈ the entire quoted backlog. **No customer is ever named**, the letters are **inconsistent between the 20-F and the Q2 6-K** for the same 2025-12-31 receivable, and **Customer E carries 29% of net AR on 14% of quarterly revenue** with no investment-grade description **[P]**.

**Supplier.** *"We currently rely on Nvidia for the GPU chips we use"* **[P]**. And **Nebius discloses no material non-cancellable GPU purchase commitment** — 20-F Note 11 lists only ~$6.8M/yr of power and utilities commitments. **That cuts both ways:** no CoreWeave-style multi-billion unconditional obligation if demand cracks, but **no contractual claim on NVIDIA supply either.** The "5 GW by 2030" partnership contains no minimum allocation, no committed volumes and no take-or-pay.

**NVIDIA's disclosed preference.** NVIDIA's own 10-Q states that AI clouds *"currently lack the ability to secure long-term infrastructure contracts and investment-grade financing capacity,"* and that it is providing guarantees and credit support — entering guarantees with SB Energy capped at **$105 billion** for capacity exclusively hosting NVIDIA compute under 20-year leases to OpenAI. It separately expects **large cloud service provider customers and investment-grade enterprises to secure land, power and shell independently. Nebius is in the second bucket.** NVIDIA also discloses backstop agreements with "AI clouds" generally — **whether Nebius has one is disclosed by neither company** (caveat 6).

**Leases.** **$12,054.1M of undiscounted payments on leases executed but not yet commenced**, terms to twelve years — **larger than the debt**, starting before the revenue it supports, with **most counterparties unnamed in any filing**.

**Internal control.** **Material weaknesses in ICFR identified as of 2025-12-31**, including revenue recognition at TripleTen; remediation "ongoing" with "no assurance" **[P]**. A company with $37.5B of RPO and $6.0B of deferred revenue running on remediating controls is a genuine audit-quality risk.

**Governance.** **Class B carries ten votes to Class A's one.** **Arkady Volozh held 28,655,509 Class B shares — 85.56% of the class and 51.62% of total voting power — at 2026-03-31**, via a Cayman trustee **[P]**. Arithmetic on the AGM share counts plus the August issuance suggests his personal majority may have slipped to roughly **48.7%** — *my derivation, not a company statement; the 10:1 structure is unchanged regardless* (caveat 7). A Dutch N.V. with FPI accommodations layers Nasdaq governance exemptions on top.

**Regulatory.** GPU export controls; a proposed BIS rule requiring IaaS providers to verify foreign customers' beneficial ownership **[P]**. **On the Russia legacy: the 20-F discloses no residual exposure, no surviving indemnity and no Russia-specific risk factor** — on the evidence of a risk-factor section running to dozens of pages, that chapter is closed.

**Skew — unusual, and worth stating precisely.** This distribution is **wide and close to symmetric in probability, but asymmetric in mechanism.** Unlike a levered developer, the bear case is **not a solvency event**: the anchors keep paying for delivered capacity, there is no rating to lose, and pro-forma the balance sheet is roughly net-cash. The floor is a **de-rated growth multiple, not zero**.

**But two features make the left tail faster and fatter than the right.** First, **the bull and bear resolve on the same variable at the same time** — asset turn improving *is* depreciation arriving, so the quarter that vindicates the thesis is the quarter that tests it. Second, **the operating-cash-flow signal inverts the usual order**: prepayment-funded OCF means deceleration shows in cash *before* revenue, so the market can learn the bear case is right while the income statement still looks excellent. That is why the history already contains a **−17.01% single session and a −32.06% five-day** inside six months.

## 10. Sector bottleneck / structural analysis

**The binding constraint has migrated, and knowing which one binds now is most of the analysis.**

**It was GPUs. It is now power — and increasingly memory.** NVIDIA's supply commitments rose **$119B → $279B**, "primarily related to the procurement of memory" **[P]**. The supply side is expanding aggressively, which *reduces* the zero-sum allocation dynamic that defined 2024–25. Being named in the select Vera Rubin allocation tier alongside CoreWeave and Lambda **[T]** is a real if soft advantage, and the **+20% increase effective 2026-10-01** is the observable footprint of a constraint that still binds somewhere.

**Power is now the constraint, and the Oracle event is the proof.** On 2026-09-24 Oracle served a **force-majeure notice** on Blue Owl's Stack Infrastructure over **Project Jupiter** — a 2.45 GW New Mexico campus — seeking the right to defer rent if the site misses its 2028 opening, after **New Mexico rejected the gas pipeline** intended to supply on-site fuel cells **[T]**. The market read it in **credit**, not equity: ~$18B of campus bank debt traded to **89–91 cents**.

**The read-through for Nebius is genuinely two-sided, and the direction is favourable.** Negative: it establishes that offtake contracts are less unconditional than "contracted revenue from investment-grade customers" implies, and it raises the cost of the project debt Nebius needs. Positive, and larger: **Nebius states that over 75% of its contracted capacity is *owned* rather than colocated** **[P]**, so it is structurally the **Oracle-side party** (offtaker/operator), not the Stack-side party bearing the delay. The cohort's price action agrees — since 2026-08-01, **NBIS +9.1%** while the developer-lessor complex fell hard (CIFR −30.7%, CORZ −27.6%, WULF −19.7%, APLD −16.8%), and **Oracle is −52.9% over one year**.

**The third constraint is capital, and it is tightening.** Data-center ABS is ~**$25B** against take-out needs approaching **~$300B** **[T]**; JPMorgan estimates $4.1 trillion of AI-related debt issuance through 2030. With the 30-year at ~5.61% and >75% implied odds of an October hike, **the discount rate, the customer's funding cost and the issuer's funding cost are now the same variable** — so rising rates are not an independent risk but a *correlated* one, hitting customer, lender and issuer simultaneously.

## 12. Asymmetry verdict

**Floor: supported but distant, $60–110.** Not a solvency floor — Microsoft and Meta pay for delivered capacity, there is no rating to lose, and pro-forma the balance sheet is roughly net-cash. It is a **multiple floor** on a business that grows without earning.

Unrated, the warning sign is **a failed deal, not a downgrade**.

**What has to be true:** asset turn improves ~3.3x; Meta's $12B lands clean in H1 2027; capital markets stay open on a **~$250B pipeline that is ~20% funded**; and scarcity pricing survives three funded competitors. **Four independent conditions, and the price assumes all four.**

## 18. Final thoughts & conclusion

**Nebius is the highest-quality business in the neocloud cohort on every axis except the one that decides the equity.** Its customers are AAA and Aa3 and they pay in advance. Its backlog is filed and audited rather than marketed — a distinction the adversarial pass went looking to demolish and instead confirmed. Its balance sheet is roughly net-cash against CoreWeave's $46B of net debt. It has demonstrated actual pricing power, twice, in a business the market assumes is commoditising. And it has been the best capital-markets operator in its sector, inventing a structure that borrows against its customers' credit instead of its own.

**And it has spent $8.1 billion in six months on an asset base that produced $471.7M of depreciation against a $1,026M straight-line charge.** Everything unresolved about this equity follows from that ratio.

### Edge — what do you believe that the crowd doesn't?

> The consensus argument for owning NBIS is hypergrowth plus counterparty quality: 454% revenue growth, Microsoft and Meta prepaying, a 41% adjusted-EBITDA margin. Every element is true and primary-sourced. **What the consensus does not price is that the metric carrying the argument is structurally incapable of showing the thing that matters.** Adjusted EBITDA excludes D&A, and **D&A is currently running at 46% of the straight-line charge** on the company's own five-year life because more than half the asset base is not in service. Strip further: **share-based compensation is ~49% of that adjusted EBITDA.** The margin the bull case quotes is calculated before the two largest costs of being this company. The forward arithmetic is the edge: at FY2025's asset turn of 0.146x and a 41% margin, Nebius generates **$0.060 of EBITDA per dollar of gross PP&E against a $0.200 depreciation charge — EBITDA is 30% of depreciation** — and GAAP breakeven requires asset turn of roughly **0.49x, a 3.3x improvement**. At the current $5.7B/quarter capex pace, gross PP&E passes $25B, and fully in service that is **~$5B/year of depreciation against $3.0B of ARR**.
>
> **So the differentiated call is this: the bull and bear cases are not two scenarios — they are the same event observed at two different times, and the market is pricing only the first half of it.** The quarter that proves the model works is the same quarter that starts the depreciation clock, and there is no path to learning one without the other. A holder who waits for "profitability" will watch adjusted EBITDA look excellent while GAAP losses widen, because the asset base entering service raises the numerator and the denominator together. **The variable to underwrite is asset turn and connected megawatts. It is not the margin, not ARR, and not the backlog.** Layer on a second mechanism nobody is watching: **operating cash flow is prepayment, not earnings.** Deferred revenue went $1,577.5M → $5,975.2M in six months against ">$9B of 2026 prepayments" guided. Prepayments are cash-positive on receipt and cash-neutral on recognition — so **OCF converges toward EBITDA the moment the prepayment book stops growing, and deceleration in new signings appears in cash before it appears in revenue.** That inverts the usual order in which an equity telegraphs trouble.
>
> **What this Edge explicitly does not claim.** It does not claim demand fails — hyperscaler capex is ~$720–745B and rising. It does not claim a customer defaults — Microsoft and Meta are among the best credits on earth. It does not claim fraud or aggressive accounting; Nebius at five years is **less** aggressive than CoreWeave at six, and its RPO is audited where the cohort's is marketed. And it does not claim the business cannot work. **It claims the market is underwriting a return on capital that the reported metrics cannot yet evidence, in a window where the evidence is about to arrive either way.**

### Tripwire — the falsification trigger and the committed action

**(1) Exit trigger — operating cash flow decelerating in any quarter while revenue accelerates.** That single divergence is the earliest observable that the prepayment engine is slowing, and because of the inverted signal order it precedes every other symptom by a quarter or more. **Decide before the print, not after.**

**(2) Slower trigger — any disclosed service credit, tranche termination or delivery-milestone remedy** under the Microsoft or Meta agreements. Both contracts carry them; **neither counterparty needs to default for the revenue to be reduced** — and a remedy event would strike revenue *and* the secured facility's collateral simultaneously.

**(3) Upward falsifier — re-underwrite, do not exit.** If two consecutive quarterly prints show revenue growth with **operating cash flow growing at least as fast**, *and* **D&A rising toward the straight-line rate without adjusted-EBITDA margin compressing below ~35%**, the capital-return concern is **falsified on its own terms** — that combination can only happen if the asset base is entering service *and* filling *at* price. **Committed action: re-underwrite upward.**

### Time-horizon fit

**The thesis needs 4–6 quarters.** Q3 and Q4 2026 show whether D&A steps up and whether the price increase holds; the ~900 MW connected target resolves at year-end; Meta's $12B tranche deploys through H1 2027. **But the falsifying signal can arrive in a single print** — OCF and revenue diverge on one cash-flow statement. That mismatch punishes patience asymmetrically and makes this a poor fit for a horizon shorter than a year in either direction.

### Conviction-appropriate sizing

Framework-level considerations only. **~71% of daily variance is not company-specific** (R² 0.707), so most days this is the neocloud complex rather than Nebius. **59% of revenue sits with three customers none of whom is named**, one holding 29% of receivables with no investment-grade description, so standard counterparty analysis is **impossible rather than incomplete**. Realized volatility is **70.9% (30d) / 131.6% (63d)** with a demonstrated **−17.01%** single session, and founder control runs **51.62% of votes on 11.33% of the economics**. Binary-adjacent exposure with an unassessable counterparty and a wide two-sided distribution warrants smaller or hedged positioning than franchise quality alone suggests. Note separately that **skew is flat (put-call IV ratio 0.9999)** — downside convexity is priced unusually cheaply against the realised left tail.

### What kills the thesis fastest

**Operating cash flow decelerating while revenue accelerates.** The highest-impact near-term gate, the leading indicator of the §8 pre-mortem, and the one signal the prepayment structure makes visible *before* the income statement. Secondary accelerant: **a Meta or Microsoft tranche slipping in H1 2027**, which strikes revenue and the secured facility's collateral at once.

### Where the price sits relative to the thesis

**Expensive on every honest denominator; defensible only on guidance not yet delivered.** At **20–23x FY2026E revenue** and **22.7x** a one-month-annualised ARR, against CoreWeave at ~12.3x trailing on 5.6x the revenue, the price embeds hypergrowth continuing for three more years *and* the capital base beginning to earn. Justifying today's EV at a 15x 2029 exit needs **~$11B of 2029 revenue undiscounted, ~$15.5B discounted at 12%** — 3.4x to 4.8x the FY2026 midpoint. **There is no valuation cushion, and the sell-side agrees only in name: $415 and $84 are not a consensus.**

Nebius has built the best-financed, cleanest-balance-sheet business in a sector about to find out whether its assets earn their cost. The growth is real, the customers are excellent, and the backlog is honest. **The open question is not whether Nebius can sell compute — it plainly can — but whether $25B of GPUs bought at scarcity prices will still be earning when they are fully depreciated.** The next two cash-flow statements will say more than the next four revenue lines.
