**The presentational point that changes the read, and it is favourable.** Bitdeer books **proceeds from selling mined bitcoin in INVESTING, not operating**, and deducts mined-coin revenue as a non-cash item — so reported OCF **excludes essentially all cash the mining business generates.** Adding the coin sales back:

- **Q1'26:** OCF −$346.9M + $206.8M of coin sales = **−$140.1M**
- **Q2'26:** OCF −$158.5M + **$195.5M** of coin sales = **+$37.0M**

**Operations plus coin sales turned cash-positive this quarter for the first time in the record this file covers.** That is a real finding and it belongs at the top of the cash section, not in a footnote. It does **not** close the funding gap: capex was **$266.0M** ($150.0M SEALMINER production, $116.0M datacenter/GPU/tariffs and freight), so **FCF was −$424.5M**, or **−$229.0M** including coin sales.

**Reconciling against the deck's own narrative, as §I.3(b) requires.** There is no "record demand with a lengthening cycle" contradiction here — the cycle shortened and the cash improved. The contradiction is elsewhere: **a company reporting its first positive operations-plus-coin-sales quarter, a $78.4M debt reduction and a $198.6M cash build filed a $1.0bn ATM the same morning.** Those two facts are consistent only if management sees a call on capital materially larger than the ~$500M of remaining Tydal capex it keeps quoting.
