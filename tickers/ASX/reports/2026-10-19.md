# ASX — ASE Technology Holding Co., Ltd.

TWSE: 3711 · NYSE: ASX (ADS; 1 ADS = 2 common shares) · Kaohsiung, Taiwan · FY ends December 31

*The world's largest chip packager and tester, re-rated from a cyclical OSAT to an AI compounder in twelve months — on a margin ramp its own management calls above "structural", partly paid for in advance by customers it does not name.*

*Contracted/backlog is distinguished from recognized revenue throughout; all point-in-time figures dated inline.*

## Executive summary — investment thesis

ASE is the largest outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) company — roughly twice Amkor's packaging-and-test revenue — plus an electronics-manufacturing arm (USI) that adds a third of revenue at a ~2–3% operating margin. AI has turned it from a mid-cycle packager into the main overflow and partner capacity for the back half of TSMC's CoWoS process: its leading-edge advanced packaging and test (LEAP) revenue went from ~US$0.6B (2024) to ~US$1.6B (2025) to "tracking ahead" of US$3.5B for 2026, and management aims to **double it again in 2027** [P/T]. Packaging-and-test (ATM) gross margin has risen from 23.8% (FY2025) to 27.3% (2Q26), with 28–29% guided for 3Q26 and 4Q26 expected to break the 30% "structural ceiling" [P/T]. The stock followed: the ADS went from a $12.46 close (2025-10-22) to $47.60 (2026-10-13) and settled at **$39.53 on 2026-10-16** after a three-session, sector-wide 17% drop; the local line settled at **NT$670 on 2026-10-19** [P bars]. At that price ASE trades at ~49x trailing EPS and ~20x a bullish broker's 2027 estimate, against 6.5–7.6x at its last earnings peak in 2021–22. **The debate is durability, not demand:** whether the AI margin is franchise earnings or contracted shortage rent.

**Reasons to own, each with its counterweight.** (1) Execution is outrunning guidance — 3Q26 ATM revenue rose 20.1% QoQ against an 11–13% guide, and testing (~21% pre-tax margin, a third of ATM profit) is growing faster than packaging — but management itself calls the coming >30% ATM margin above its "structural ceiling", which concedes the peak is cyclical. (2) Customers are committing in advance: "other liabilities", where ASE books advance payments under long-term agreements, rose from NT$7.1B (12/2024) to NT$60.9B (3/2026) — but those advances flatter operating cash flow (67% of 1Q26 OCF) and will be worked off against future revenue at undisclosed prices. (3) TSMC still calls the back-end shortage "quite big" and is widening what it hands to OSATs — but ASE's US$10.5B 2026 capex is ~4x its depreciation, so the 2027–28 depreciation step-up lands whatever happens to pricing.

**What has to be true.** LEAP roughly doubles in 2027 *and* ATM margin holds near 30% as depreciation steps up *and* customers keep renewing advance commitments, so that reported cash flow is not just the prepayment cycle.

**Bottom line.** A genuinely strong business at a price that assumes the strongest year of its history repeats. The prepayments make the demand more certain and the margins less so; the market is paying for both.

## 1. Business model & role in the value chain

ASE packages and tests chips designed by others and made mostly by TSMC: it bumps wafers, mounts dies on substrates, wires or flips them, assembles multi-die modules and runs wafer sort and final test. It earns a per-unit service fee, so revenue scales with unit volume times package complexity, not with chip prices. USI, its EMS subsidiary, builds system-in-package modules and boards (mostly for smartphones and wearables) on thin margins. A small materials line supplies substrates and leadframes.

**Position in the value chain — what ASE is NOT.** It is not a foundry: it does not make wafers, and the highest-value AI packaging step — Chip-on-Wafer (CoW), stacking the GPU die and HBM on a silicon interposer — is mostly done inside TSMC. ASE takes the "on-Substrate" (oS) step that follows under a TSMC process licence, a growing slice of CoW as overflow (~US$300M of "full-process" revenue in 2026, ~10% of LEAP [T]), and the testing. It is not a substrate maker (Ibiden, Unimicron supply that) and not an equipment maker (Advantest, Disco sell to it). TSMC's 20-F-acknowledged role is "non-exclusive preferred provider" partner — an alliance "that may be terminated at any time" [P].

**Which segment is the equity story vs. ballast.** ATM is the equity story (66% of 2Q26 revenue, ~93% of operating income). Within ATM, LEAP is the growth and testing is the margin. EMS is ballast that grows with Apple's build cycle and adds no multiple.

## 4. Technological moat & competitive technologies

The problem ASE solves is joining several dies (logic plus HBM, or chiplets) into one high-bandwidth package and proving it works, at volume.

| Approach | How it works | Champion(s) | Trade-off vs ASE's approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundry-integrated 2.5D (CoWoS-S/-L/-R) | Silicon interposer or local silicon bridges, built in the foundry; CoW in-house, oS outsourced | TSMC | Highest yield and integration; TSMC keeps CoW. ASE does oS under licence and a small, growing share of CoW [T] |
| OSAT 2.5D / fan-out (FOCoS, VIPack, bridge fan-out) | Fan-out redistribution layers or embedded bridges replace the silicon interposer | **ASE**, Amkor (HDFO/S-SWIFT), JCET (XDFOI) | Cheaper and less capital per unit; coarser interconnect, so it loses on the densest HBM-heavy GPUs. Reported in AMD, NVIDIA Vera, Broadcom and AWS designs [T] |
| Embedded bridge in substrate (EMIB/EMIB-T) | Small silicon bridges inside an organic substrate; no full interposer | Intel Foundry | Capacity and cost alternative to CoWoS; reported prospects Google TPU, AWS [T]. ASE calls it "not a zero-sum game" and collaborates [T3] |
| Panel-level (FOPLP / CoPoS) | Rectangular panels replace 300mm wafers for large packages | ASE (310×310mm, volume Q1 2027, slipped from end-2026), TSMC CoPoS (volume 2H28–29) [T] | Cost-down path; warpage and yield unproven. ASE calls its line "complementary" to CoPoS |
| Substrate-less (CoWoP) | CoW mounted directly on a PCB, removing the ABF substrate | NVIDIA; project reportedly led by SPIL (ASE) [T] | Removes the substrate bottleneck; would favour ASE if SPIL leads. Early |
| 3D hybrid bonding (SoIC) / HBM back-end | Copper-to-copper die stacking in the fab; HBM stacked at the memory maker | TSMC, SK hynix, Samsung | Moves the highest-value integration upstream; HBM hybrid bonding slipped to ~2027+ [T] |
| Glass-core substrates | Glass replaces the organic core | Absolics/SKC, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Intel (~2030) [T] | A materials change OSATs adopt, not one that displaces them |
| In-house vs outsourced test | Wafer sort, final test, burn-in, system-level test | KYEC, ASE, IDMs | KYEC's ~1,500W burn-in ovens are a cost edge on AI GPUs [P]; NVIDIA pre-paid KYEC, not ASE, for US test |

**Where the industry is converging.** Foundry-led 2.5D/3D (CoWoS-L, then SoIC and CoPoS) remains the leading edge through ~2028, and the shortage is forcing a multi-source "second supply chain" around it: OSAT oS, some CoW, Intel EMIB-T, and interposers from GlobalFoundries (TSMC, 10-15) [P/T]. The 2024→2026 direction is **widening** outsourcing — oS only, then CoW overflow.

**Defensibility.** Patents and process know-how in fan-out, bumping and test are real but not exclusive — Amkor and JCET practise every OSAT technique ASE does. The barriers are elsewhere: (1) **qualification** — customers qualify each facility separately [P], so moving a product costs quarters; (2) **capital** — ASE's US$10.5B 2026 capex is ~3.5x Amkor's US$2.5–3.0B [P/T]; (3) **turnkey breadth** — bump, package, test and SiP under one roof; (4) **TSMC proximity** — Taiwan sites next to CoWoS fabs. Who controls the substitutes: TSMC (CoW, SoIC, CoPoS) and Intel (EMIB) — both foundries, both able to keep work in-house.

**Verdict: technological moat — *emerging (optionality)* at the AI leading edge, *durable* in mainstream OSAT.** ASE's AI exposure is mostly an integration and capacity layer on TSMC-licensed process; the proprietary pieces (FOCoS/VIPack, the panel line, the CoWoP lead, test know-how) are real but not yet the main AI bill of materials.

## 6. Secular / thematic positioning

ASE's exposure to AI is **second-derivative**: it is paid per package, so its revenue tracks accelerator *units* × package complexity (interposer area, HBM stacks, test minutes) × its share of the outsourced back-end. It is **first-derivative to the back-end shortage**: when TSMC cannot do all of CoWoS in-house, ASE's share and pricing rise together. That is why the ramp is so sharp and why it is two-sided — the shortage creates both the volume and the "very friendly pricing environment" management describes [T].

Three secular legs sit underneath, in descending order of AI dependence: (1) **LEAP** (2.5D, fan-out, bumping for AI accelerators and custom ASICs) — directly AI; (2) **test** — rises with die count, die size and burn-in time, so it benefits from AI without depending on the CoWoS shortage; (3) **mainstream packaging and EMS** — smartphones, PCs, auto and industrial, cyclical and recovering ("general" ATM guided +20% for 2026, up from 13% [P]).

Treat ~NT$54B (≈US$1.7B) as contracted pre-funding whose price terms and offset schedule are unknown — and remember it is **cash received, not revenue recognized**.

**Headline optics distortions.** (1) **Operating cash flow overstates the core cash engine.** Advances enter OCF: +NT$32.0B in FY2025 (22% of OCF) and +NT$24.4B in 1Q26 (67%) [P]. When they are worked off, revenue arrives with no new cash. (2) **2Q26 net income includes ~NT$4.6B of non-operating items**, including NT$3.6B of FX-hedging gains [P]. (3) **3Q26's revenue beat is EMS-heavy**, so the consolidated margin will look weaker than the ATM story. (4) **The trailing P/E (~49x) understates the momentum and the forward P/E (~20x 2027E) assumes it** — neither is the right anchor alone.

**Unit economics and profit bridge.** ATM gross margin rose 4.7 points from 22.6% (1Q25) to 27.3% (2Q26) while ATM depreciation and rental reached NT$16.9B a quarter in 2Q26 [P]. The bridge is mostly **price and mix**, not volume leverage: computing rose from 24% to 30% of ATM revenue, wirebond fell from 28% to 24%, testing rose to 19%, and management describes pricing as "very friendly" [P/T]. Wirebonders fell (25,156 → 24,815) while testers rose 23% — the capacity is being rebuilt toward AI, not added on top. A price/mix-to-volume ratio is not derivable from disclosures.

**The depreciation step-up (my estimate, illustrative).** 2026 capex of ~US$10.5B splits ~US$6.5B equipment and ~US$4B facilities [T]. At ~5–7-year equipment lives and ~20–30-year buildings (assumptions, not ASE's disclosed policy), that adds roughly **NT$34–47B a year of D&A** once in service — 50–70% on top of FY2025's NT$67.4B. On BofA's 2027E net income (~NT$154B), that is a 20–30% headwind that LEAP growth has to outrun.

## 8. Risks

**Pre-mortem — the single most likely *fundamental* reason the equity loses most of its value.** Not zero: ASE is a profitable, diversified franchise with a dividend, and solvency is not in question. The realistic large-loss mechanism is **an AI capacity overhang that arrives through the balance sheet rather than the order book**: TSMC's CoWoS expansion (and Intel EMIB-T as a second source) narrows the back-end shortage in 2027–28; TSMC keeps CoW and SoIC; ASE's customers finish working off their advances, so reported revenue holds while cash inflow drops; the ~NT$34–47B annual depreciation step-up from the 2026 build lands at the same time; ATM margin falls back toward 24–26%; and the market re-prices ASE from an AI multiple back toward its 2023–24 range (16–22x trough earnings) on lower earnings. Leading indicator: advances stop growing while capex stays high.

**Named risks.**

- **Customer concentration.** One unnamed customer (with its OEMs) was 25.0% of FY2025 revenue (29.3% in FY2024); top five 46.5% [P]. Most likely Apple (packaging plus EMS), but **unconfirmed**. NVIDIA, the main AI end-customer, reaches ASE mostly through TSMC and is not named in either company's filings.
- **TSMC as partner and rival.** The alliance "may be terminated at any time"; TSMC's InFO is named as competition [P]. TSMC decides what it outsources.
- **Undisclosed advance terms.** If the advances carry fixed prices or volume commitments, they cap the margin upside; if they are offset against fees (as KYEC's are), they depress 2027–28 cash flow.
- **Leverage into a capex peak.** Net debt/equity 0.40 → 0.47 in one quarter; NT$155.2B of long-term debt due in 1–3 years; cross-default clauses [P]. BofA flagged a possible 2H26 bond or equity raise [T].
- **Geopolitics.** 58.9% of revenue from Taiwan operations [P]; US–PRC export controls on advanced computing; semiconductor tariffs the US "intends to increase over time" [P]; controlling stake in USI Shanghai (SSE: 601231).
- **Section 232.** OSAT services treatment under the semiconductor tariff and the US–Taiwan quota is unknown.
- **Governance and related parties.** Chairman Jason Chang beneficially owns 21.35% [P]; repeated appraisal-priced property purchases from equity-method affiliate Hung Ching (NT$5.26B plant 2024; NT$4.23B Chung-Li building and land approved 2025-11) [P]. Holding-company share pledges unverified.

## 10. Sector bottleneck / structural analysis

The binding constraint in AI hardware is **advanced packaging capacity**, specifically the interposer-based 2.5D integration of GPU dies and HBM. TSMC's CEO (2026-10-15) says the gap is "still quite big", that he cannot tell whether it is widening because customers keep raising demand, and that there is "a little bit shortage on the back end" — which TSMC is addressing partly through interposers from GlobalFoundries [P]. Trade estimates have TSMC's CoWoS capacity roughly doubling by 2028 [T3]; a June 2026 estimate of the shortage narrowing from ~20% to ~10% by end-2026 [T3] is contradicted by TSMC's own October commentary.

ASE monetizes the bottleneck in three ways: **capacity** (oS and overflow CoW), **price** ("very friendly pricing"), and **test time** (AI dies take longer to test and burn in). Secondary constraints — ABF substrates, HBM supply, testers — sit with ASE's suppliers and customers, not with ASE. The structural question is how long the bottleneck lasts relative to ASE's capex cycle: a shortage that eases in 2028 meets capacity ASE is buying in 2026.

## 15. Investment thesis (synthesis)

- **The AI ramp is real and ahead of plan** — LEAP from US$0.6B to >US$3.5B in two years, 3Q26 ATM +20.1% QoQ against an 11–13% guide. *Counterweight:* management's own "structural ceiling" of 30% ATM margin is being exceeded, which by definition is the cyclical part of the earnings.
- **Customers are pre-committing capacity** — ~NT$54B of advances under long-term agreements, comparable to NVIDIA's Amkor deal, makes 2027 volumes more certain. *Counterweight:* the same advances inflate operating cash flow now and depress it later, and their price terms — which may cap the margin upside — are not disclosed.
- **Testing is a durable, high-margin pool** that grows with AI die count without depending on the CoWoS shortage. *Counterweight:* NVIDIA chose to pre-fund KYEC, not ASE, for US test capacity.
- **ASE is the scale leader** with twice Amkor's revenue and margins ten points higher. *Counterweight:* its AI position is an integration layer on TSMC process; TSMC decides the handoff.

**What has to be true:** LEAP doubles in 2027, ATM margin holds near 30% through the depreciation step-up, and customers keep renewing advance commitments.

## 18. Final thoughts & conclusion

ASE is executing as well as any company in the AI hardware chain: the AI ramp is ahead of plan, testing compounds quietly, the balance sheet is sound, and TSMC is handing off more, not less. The stock has priced that and more — from ~22x trough earnings a year ago to ~49x trailing and ~20x a bullish 2027 estimate, against a 6.5–7.6x multiple at its last peak. The filings add something the price does not reflect: a new, undisclosed-terms layer of customer advance payments that makes the demand more certain, the cash flow less clean, and the margin ceiling a negotiated number rather than a market one.

### Edge — what do you believe that the crowd doesn't?

> The consensus reads ASE's AI ramp as a structural margin story: leading-edge advanced packaging and test (LEAP) revenue from ~US$1.6B (2025) to >US$3.5B (2026) with a stated aim to double again in 2027, lifting ATM gross margin from 23.8% (FY2025) to 27.3% (2Q26), 28–29% guided for 3Q26 and >30% expected in 4Q26 — and it is paying ~20x a 2027 estimate that is 3.7x FY2025 earnings, against 6.5–7.6x at the last earnings peak. **What the consensus does not price is that a material part of this ramp is pre-sold, and pre-sold capacity has a different earnings shape from scarce capacity.** ASE's FY2025 20-F newly discloses long-term customer agreements with advance payments, and the liability lines that hold them rose from ~NT$7B (12/2024) to ~NT$61B (3/2026) non-current — roughly NT$54B (≈US$1.7B) of cash received for capacity not yet delivered, from customers ASE does not name, at prices it does not disclose.
>
> **So the differentiated call is this: the variable that decides the equity is not the LEAP headline — it is what the advances do next.** They have flattered reported cash flow (22% of FY2025 operating cash flow, 67% of 1Q26's; ex-advances, 1Q26 OCF was ~NT$12B against NT$44B of capex payments), and as they are worked off against future revenue, 2027–28 revenue arrives without matching cash — just as the US$10.5B 2026 build adds an estimated NT$34–47B a year of depreciation. And capacity that customers pre-pay is capacity whose price was fixed when it was scarce: management's own "structural ceiling" of 30% ATM margin, which 4Q26 is expected to exceed, is more likely the negotiated top of a cycle than the floor of a new one. The market is capitalising contracted shortage pricing as franchise pricing.
>
> **What this Edge explicitly does not claim.** It does not claim AI demand fails — TSMC's CEO called the back-end gap "still quite big" on 2026-10-15. It does not claim TSMC is reclaiming work — outsourcing is widening. It does not claim the advances are bad: they reduce volume risk, and they are the reason this is a re-rating of a profitable franchise rather than a capacity gamble. And it does not cover testing, which grows with die count independently of the CoWoS shortage. **It claims that the margin and cash-flow path the price assumes is the least certain part of the story, and that the evidence on it — the advance balance and the post-ceiling margin — arrives over the next four prints.**

### Tripwires — the falsification triggers and committed actions

- **(1)** **ASE withdraws or cuts its aim to double LEAP revenue in 2027** at any results call, **or** 2026 LEAP finishes below US$3.5B, **or** 2027 LEAP finishes below 2x 2026, **or** ASE or TSMC discloses a reduction in TSMC's handoff of CoW or on-substrate work to ASE/SPIL. **Committed action: re-underwrite** — the demand leg of every scenario above rests on it.
- **(2)** **The advance balance declines for two consecutive quarters while quarterly equipment capex stays at or above US$1.5B** — measured on other current plus other non-current liabilities in the reviewed financial statements. Customers would be consuming pre-paid capacity without renewing it while ASE keeps building. **Committed action: re-underwrite toward the bear case.**
- **(3)** **ATM gross margin below 26% for two consecutive quarters while ATM revenue is still growing year on year** — depreciation outrunning price, not a demand drop. **Committed action: exit or re-underwrite; do not wait for the full-year print.**
- **(4)** **Any primary equity issuance** (cash capital increase, GDR or placement — excluding conversion of the existing NT$927.20 convertible and employee awards) **or net debt/equity above 0.65 at any quarter-end.** **Committed action: re-underwrite** — the build would be outrunning both the cash flow and the customer funding.
- **(5)** **[UPWARD — re-underwrite, do not exit]** if **ATM gross margin is at or above 30% in each of two consecutive quarters in 2027 *and* operating cash flow excluding the change in other operating liabilities exceeds PP&E cash capex over the same two quarters**, the Edge is falsified on its own terms: margins would be holding after the ceiling *and* the core business would be self-funding without the advances. **Committed action: re-underwrite upward.**

### Time-horizon fit

The thesis resolves over **12–18 months**: the 4Q26 margin and 2027 guide (Feb 2027), the reviewed advance balances each quarter, and the 2027 LEAP delivery through the FY2027 print (~Feb 2028). Shorter horizons are dominated by AI-complex beta (10-14 → 10-16).

### Conviction-appropriate sizing

Revenue is diversified, but the **thesis is single-variable**: almost all of the re-rating rests on the AI back-end shortage persisting through ASE's capex cycle. That warrants treating it as a concentrated theme position, not a diversified industrial — framework-level guidance, not a recommendation.

### What kills the thesis fastest

A 4Q26 or 1Q27 ATM margin guide that **stalls below 30%** while capex stays high — the fastest route to Tripwire #3 and the pre-mortem's leading edge.

### Where the price sits relative to the thesis

**Pricing the optimistic case.** At ~20x 2027E and ~13.5x 2028E, NT$670 assumes the 2028 bull-case earnings arrive and hold; the probability-weighted value (~NT$605) sits ~10% below.

### What to monitor next

- **~2026-10-29** — 3Q26 results: 4Q ATM margin guide, the LEAP 2027 reaffirmation, first 2027 capex signal.
- **~November 2026** — 2Q26 and 3Q26 reviewed financial statements: the advance balance (Tripwire #2) and the unexplained NT$13B of 2Q26 bonds.
- **~late Oct 2026** — Amkor 3Q26 and Advantest results: the peer margin and tester-demand read.
- **~February 2027** — 4Q26 results: formal 2027 LEAP and capex guidance; whether ATM margin exceeded 30%.
- **Q1 2027** — panel-level line in volume.

### Bottom line

ASE has earned its re-rating operationally; the question is whether it has earned it permanently. The demand side is as good as it gets and partly pre-paid. The earnings side — margins above the company's own stated ceiling, a depreciation step-up already bought, and advances that will be worked off at undisclosed prices — is where the price is ahead of the evidence.

**Adversarial review (§L) — what it found and how it was reconciled.** The red team was dispatched against the *draft* Edge, which claimed ASE was funding its AI capacity largely on its own balance sheet while NVIDIA pre-paid rivals, and that the CoWoS shortage was closing. **It found a real problem on both counts.** (1) The FY2025 20-F's new Note 37(e) discloses customer advance payments under long-term agreements, and the liability lines holding them rose ~NT$54B from 12/2024 to 3/2026 — contradicting "self-funded" from primary source. (2) TSMC's CEO said on 2026-10-15 that the gap is "still quite big", contradicting the trade-press "20% → 10%" narrowing estimate. It also corrected the FY2025 ATM gross margin base (23.8%, not ~22%), flagged that "≥US$7.5B" for 2027 LEAP is a press derivation of management's "double", and that the draft upward tripwire would have fired immediately. **Reconciliation, not averaging:** the four research agents had all framed ASE through the margin ramp and the TSMC overflow; the red team's finding was load-bearing and changed the Edge's mechanism. The published Edge is rebuilt around what the filings do show — the advances, their effect on cash flow and their likely effect on pricing — and the tripwires are re-based on management's own wording and on the advance balance. The red team's steelman (test as a non-overflow profit pool; most LEAP is oS and wafer sort rather than overflow CoW) is reflected in §1, §5 and the Edge's explicit exclusions.
