## Executive summary — investment thesis

LPKF sells LIDE (Laser-Induced Deep Etching) systems that structure glass and form through-glass vias for the glass-core substrates AI packaging is moving toward, alongside a shrinking legacy laser business — PCB prototyping, plastics welding, laser direct structuring and solar scribing — that produced €115.3m of revenue in FY2025 and €36.5m in H1 2026 **[P]**. **The debate is financing and durability, not demand**: glass-core adoption is broadening, but LPKF's cash runs short before its first production LIDE order turns into revenue, and the method it sells is less proprietary than the €22–23.50 sell-side targets assume.

**Three reasons to own, each with its counterweight.** **(1)** A customer has "fully qualified" LIDE and signed a letter of intent for NEXAR LIDE systems worth a mid-single-digit €m (~3–6 systems) on 23 Sep, into a glass-core build-out now spanning Absolics, SEMCO/GlaSSEM, BOE and Intel–Lens **[P/T]** — *but* it is still a letter of intent, LPKF said on 30 Sep the orders arrive "later than originally expected" with revenue only in FY2027, and no independent source corroborates its €1.7bn-by-2030 TAM **[P]**. **(2)** LPKF holds granted LIDE patents running to ~2036–2038 and process know-how from more than 20 installed units **[P/T]** — *but* laser-modification-plus-etch is not LPKF-exclusive: Corning's patent predates LIDE's 2014 priority, the European LIDE patent survived opposition only in amended form, at least six rivals sell the method openly, and Philoptics booked production TGV tools on 29 Sep while LPKF has none **[P/T]**. **(3)** The balance sheet carries €63.9m of equity (62.8% ratio), a €32.5m syndicated facility to 2028, and guidance that implies a profitable H2 (+€3.9m to +€7.9m adjusted EBIT) **[P, derived]** — *but* liquidity at 30 Jun was ~€6.8m (€4.0m cash plus ~€2.8m undrawn), H1 free cash flow was −€11.5m, and without a new AGM LPKF can place only ~10% of its shares ex-rights, worth ~€33–37m at today's price **[P, derived]**.

**What has to be true:** the binding LIDE order lands before LPKF has to raise equity, the guided H2 turn to profit actually happens, and LIDE wins production sockets against laser-modification rivals that are already shipping — in that order.

**Bottom line.** At **€14.95 (settled 2026-10-07)** and a ~€366m market cap, roughly 60–75% of the enterprise value is a LIDE option that has not yet produced a production order. The equity's next twelve months turn on a sequence the balance sheet forces: order → H2 turn → raise terms. If the order lands first, LPKF raises at a premium or not at all; if the raise comes first, it is priced off a stock that has just been told its order slipped.

## 4. Technological moat & competing approaches

| Approach | How it works | Champions | Trade-off vs LIDE |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Laser modification + wet etch (LIDE-class)** | Ultrashort pulse modifies the glass along the via path; wet etch removes the modified zone | **LPKF LIDE** **[P]**; **Corning** line-focus damage track + acid etch (US9517963, priority Dec 2013) **[P]**; 4JET/Plan Optik VLIS; DR Laser LACE; Hymson; Philoptics; E&R; Delphi **[T/T3]** | **The same process family.** Differences are recipes, throughput and glass thickness — Philoptics shows 2.0mm on 510×515mm panels; LPKF's stated range is up to ~1mm per pulse **[P/T]** |
| Direct ultrafast ablation | Holes drilled directly by fs/ps bursts | EKSPLA, TRUMPF, EO Technics **[T3]** | No etch step, but slower at high via density, with chipping risk |
| CO₂ / UV percussion drilling | Thermal ablation | Legacy PCB-drill vendors | Cheap; microcracks and taper make it unfit for glass cores |
| Photosensitive glass | UV exposure, bake, etch the crystallized phase | Schott Foturan II **[P]** | Lithographic, but locks the customer into special glass; an RF/MEMS niche |
| Plasma / DRIE | Masked dry etch | Not verified | Slow on glass; wafer-scale only |

**Convergence.** The industry is converging on **laser modification plus wet etch** — crack-free, panel-scalable, batch etch — which TrendForce describes as having passed qualification at major IDMs **[T]**. **The convergence is the problem for LPKF**: LIDE's *category* won, but the category is not proprietary to LPKF.

**Defensibility.**

- **Patents.** The core family (WO2016041544, priority 16 Sep 2014) is granted as US11610784B2 (expiry ~2036), with JP, CN and TW equivalents; **EP3195706 was maintained in amended (B2) form** after an anonymous 2024 opposition; US11065716 (~2038) covers a surface-roughness parameter window; a Korean patent took effect 1 Sep 2025 **[P]**. **Corning's US9517963 (priority 17 Dec 2013) already claims a line-focus laser making damage tracks followed by acid etch** — earlier than LPKF's priority — so LPKF's protection rests on specific parameters and structures, not on the general method **[P]**. LPKF is litigating infringers in China (CEO letters) **[P]**; **no enforcement action against any of the six non-Chinese rivals was found**.
- **Know-how.** More than 20 LIDE units installed (as of Sep 2024), mostly R&D and pilot **[T]**; a foundry service (Vitrion); co-founder of the Fraunhofer glass-packaging consortium **[P]**.
- **Switching costs.** Real once a customer qualifies a line — the 23 Sep customer "fully qualified" LIDE — but the etch chemistry and process recipe migrate to the customer over time.
- **Capital barrier.** Low: Chinese laser makers with far larger balance sheets (Han's Laser) are entering.

**Verdict: technological moat EMERGING and narrowing — real know-how and a qualified customer, but a method that rivals practise openly and a patent position that is narrower than the general approach.**

## 6. Secular / thematic positioning

LPKF is a **second-derivative** beneficiary of AI packaging: it does not sell into today's CoWoS ramp, it sells into the next substrate generation, whose volume timing other companies control. The secular case is real — glass cores address warpage and density limits of organic substrates at the package sizes AI accelerators are heading toward — but **exposure to the theme is not capture of it**. The capture depends on LIDE staying the specified via method once lines move from pilot to production, and on LPKF surviving financially until they do.

## 10. Sector bottleneck / structural analysis

**The binding constraint on glass cores is not via-forming tools — it is downstream customer qualification and line economics.** SEMCO's line slipped after a reported reliability evaluation ran long (The Elec, 14 Aug; SEMCO disputes a failed qualification) **[T]**; Absolics needed a ₩401bn rights offering to keep going **[T]**; TSMC's near-term panel route (CoPoS) is not glass core, which it sees post-2030 **[T]**. **What has to be true:** the order beats the raise, H2 turns profitable, and LIDE holds its socket against laser-modification rivals. **Three conditions in sequence, the first of which resolves within one or two quarters.**

## 18. Final thoughts & conclusion

LPKF has done the hard technical part: a customer has fully qualified LIDE and signed a letter of intent for production systems. The sell-side has rewarded that with two Buys at €22–23.50. But since July three things have changed underneath the story — the cash bridge turned out to be far shorter than cited, the order slipped a second time, and the closest rival started booking production tools in the same process family. **The question is no longer whether LIDE works, but who pays for the wait and at what price.**

### Edge — what do you believe that the crowd doesn't?

> The sell-side calls the 30 Sep cut a timing shift and values LIDE as a near-proprietary option at €22–23.50; the prior view here held that timing was the wrong axis and that the question was whether cash plus the credit facility could bridge to a 2029 payoff. Both miss the same thing. **The bridge does not exist, so the equity raise is not a duration risk but a near-term event — and its price will be set by exactly the order news the market is waiting for.** At 30 Jun liquidity was about **€6.8m** — €4.0m of cash plus roughly €2.8m undrawn on a €12.5m cash line already ~€9.7m drawn — against an H1 burn of €11.5m, and without a new AGM LPKF can place only ~10% of its shares ex-rights (≈€33–37m at €14.95), because the June 2025 AGM refused a larger authority. The next twelve months therefore turn on a sequence: **binding order → H2 turn → raise terms.** If the order lands first, LPKF raises at a premium or not at all; if the raise comes first, it is priced off a stock that has just been told its order slipped.
>
> **And the asset being funded is less proprietary than the targets assume.** Laser-modification-plus-wet-etch is practised openly by at least six rivals; Corning's patent on the general method predates LIDE's 2014 priority; LPKF's European patent survived opposition only in amended form; and Philoptics booked production TGV tools on 29 Sep while LPKF holds a letter of intent. So even a well-sequenced outcome may fund a tool that ends up one qualified option among several rather than the standard.
>
> **What this Edge does not claim.** It does not claim glass-core demand fails — the build-out is broadening. It does not claim LPKF is insolvent — equity is €63.9m and the facility runs to 2028. It does not claim LIDE loses — a customer has qualified it. **It claims the order of events decides who captures the value, and the market is pricing the end-state while the balance sheet forces the sequence.**

### Tripwires — the falsification triggers and committed actions

- **(1)** The **binding NEXAR LIDE order** from the 23 Sep 2026 letter of intent is **not announced by 2026-12-31** — the end of the fiscal year in which management promised (23 Jul) a first production order — **or is announced below the letter's "mid-single-digit €m"**.
- **(2)** a rival TGV method (e.g., Philoptics, Han's Laser) wins a named **first HVM socket** — erodes LIDE-as-standard, the core of the moat.
- **(3)** **Financing on adverse terms:** any equity raise **priced below €12**, **or** one **larger than the 10% ex-rights authority** (a rights issue or an extraordinary general meeting), **or** any disclosed **covenant waiver, amendment or breach**, or a reduction or non-extension of the €32.5m syndicated facility.
- **(4)** the glass volume-production timeline slips materially past **2029**.
- **(5)** **H2 2026 adjusted EBIT comes in below zero** — the 30 Sep guidance implies roughly +€3.9m to +€7.9m — **or year-end 2026 liquidity** (cash plus undrawn cash facility) is **below ~€5m without a financing announcement**.
- **(6)** **Upward falsifier — re-underwrite, do not exit:** a **binding LIDE production order is announced by 2026-12-31 AND** LPKF reaches FY2026 year-end **without an equity raise, or with one priced at or above ~€20** — the financing pinch was resolved by orders rather than dilution.

### Time-horizon fit

The decisive window is **one to two quarters**: the Q3 report (29 Oct), the binding order (by 31 Dec), and the FY2026 report (~Mar 2027). The structural question — LIDE as standard or commodity — plays out over **2027–29**. A horizon shorter than the Q3 report is trading European semicap beta, not LPKF.

### Conviction-appropriate sizing

Framework-level considerations: a single unnamed customer carries the order thesis; a raise is disclosed as under evaluation; the ex-rights cap makes any larger raise contentious; and the downside resolves on one ordering event. This is a **binary-adjacent, single-counterparty-dependent** position — the profile that warrants smaller or staged exposure, ideally sized around the order and raise announcements rather than ahead of them.

### What kills the thesis fastest

**A placement announced before a binding order** — it fires the dilution logic of #3 at a likely de-rated price, confirms the sequence ran the wrong way, and arrives in a single ad-hoc release. Secondary: a Q3 report showing H2 behind the guided turn.

### Where the price sits relative to the thesis

**Fairly priced for a funded end-state; expensive for an unfunded one.** At ~€366m, 60–75% of EV is LIDE option value; the €22–23.50 targets price the end-state without its funding cost.

### What to monitor next

- **2026-10-29** — **Q3/9M report**: cash, drawn lines, covenant language, H2 trajectory.
- **2026-12-31** — **Binding NEXAR LIDE order** (the promise for this fiscal year).
- **Any time** — **an equity raise**: size, price, ex-rights or rights issue.
- **~2027-03** — **FY2026 annual report**: H2 adjusted EBIT, year-end liquidity, covenant compliance.
- **2027-06-03** — **AGM 2027**: any new capital authorisation.
- **Ongoing** — named HVM socket awards (LIDE or Philoptics/Chinese rivals); SEMCO, Absolics and BOE line timing.

### Bottom line

LPKF has proved LIDE works; it has not yet proved it can fund the wait or keep the socket. **The next ad-hoc release that matters is either a binding order or a placement — and which one comes first decides most of the equity's value over the next year.**
