## Executive summary — investment thesis

Applied Optoelectronics makes optical transceivers for AI datacenters and amplifiers for cable networks, and it grows many of its own laser chips in Sugar Land. It closed at **$40.79 on 2026-11-18**, down 81.7% from the $223.10 closing high of 2026-05-13, for a market value of about **$3.70B** on 90.77M shares [P]. The debate is no longer whether demand exists. Management reports about $450M of 1.6T orders it cannot yet ship [T]. The debate is **durability and funding**: can a supplier that holds a minority, price-capped position turn roughly $2B of capacity spending into per-share returns before equity and vendor credit run short?

**Three reasons to own, each with its counterweight:**
1. Q4 datacenter revenue is guided to $270–300M against $155.6M in Q3, with a first long-term agreement expected at $1.0–1.5B through May 2029, but that agreement covers only 10% of certain 1.6T needs, includes price cuts and carries no customer capital.
2. At $40.79 the stock trades near 2.1x annualized Q4 guided revenue and about 1.6x tangible book of $24.89 per share, but $955.9M of purchase commitments sit against about $578M of pro forma cash, and full use of the new $550M ATM at this price would issue about 14.9% more stock.
3. A US restriction on new Chinese transceiver models is advancing and could make non-Chinese supply structurally more valuable, but only 0.6% of AAOI's Q3 revenue was manufactured in the US, against 55.4% in Taiwan and 44.1% in China.

**What has to be true:** DSP supply must loosen in Q1 2027 as management expects, Texas capacity must arrive despite power delays, non-GAAP gross margin must climb from 28.7% toward the mid-30s, the East West covenants must hold from their first test on 2026-12-31, and the equity must be sold at prices that do not consume the remaining authorized shares.

**Bottom line:** the stock has already priced much of what went wrong, and our probability-weighted value of about $46 is modestly above the price, but the range is unusually wide, at $10–18 in the bear case and $90–115 in the bull case. AAOI is a financed execution bet with real demand. It is not yet a franchise, and it no longer looks like a simple scarcity peak either.

## 1. Business model & role in the value chain

AAOI designs and manufactures transceivers and the lasers and subassemblies inside them. All laser chips are made in Sugar Land using MBE and MOCVD processes, and the company says it makes the majority of the laser chips and optical components used in its products [P, FY25 10-K]. It reports **one operating segment**, so product-line gross margin is not disclosed [P]. Every datacenter margin figure in circulation is therefore an estimate.

*Source: 10-Q, 2026-11-05 [P]; YoY derived.*

**What it is not.** AAOI is not a Top-tier transceiver vendor by revenue, not a DSP maker, and not a proven merchant supplier of 200G EMLs. We found no reliable evidence that it ships 200G EMLs in volume, and its own published roadmap centres on silicon photonics [T]. The transceiver is partly an integration product built around bought DSPs and TIAs. The laser fab is the differentiated asset, but its internal/external split, merchant laser revenue and laser mix are undisclosed.

**Equity story versus ballast.** Datacenter is the equity story, and it is guided to about 71% of Q4 revenue at midpoints (derived). CATV was the profit and cash anchor of 2025 but is now the ballast: it is guided flat at $105–115M in Q4, or about 27% of revenue. Nearly all of it runs through one distributor, Digicomm, whose Q3 revenue of about $110.0M roughly equals total CATV revenue (derived) [P].

**Where it is made.** Q3 revenue by place of manufacture was Taiwan $149.4M (55.4%), China $118.9M (44.1%) and the US $1.5M (0.6%) [P]. China-made revenue is close to CATV revenue, which suggests the cable line is largely China-built, though the filing does not say so. Long-lived assets at 9/30/26 were US $433.6M, China $384.0M and Taiwan $305.5M, so the US build is visible on the balance sheet but not yet in shipments [P].

## 4. Technological moat & competitive technologies

| Approach | How it works | Champions | Trade-off vs. AAOI's approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| EML pluggable | One modulated InP laser per lane | Coherent, Lumentum | Proven at 200G per lane but InP-hungry and supply-bound. AAOI argues 8-channel 1.6T EML faces a yield "complexity wall" [T, interested party] |
| Silicon photonics plus CW laser | A CW laser feeds a photonic circuit that modulates channels | AAOI's stated roadmap; several large module makers | Uses fewer, simpler lasers, which shifts value toward the photonic chip, DSP and packaging |
| LPO / LRO | Removes or reduces the DSP | AAOI showed a 1.6T LPO module in 2024 | Would ease AAOI's DSP bottleneck, but we found no evidence of volume |
| CPO / NPO with external laser | Optics move next to the switch, fed by 300–400mW lasers | NVIDIA, Coherent, Lumentum | Removes the pluggable module but raises laser value. Incumbents hold the marquee contracts |
| USFP, 400G per lane | New pluggable form factor from NVIDIA and Cisco, announced 2026-10-12 | NVIDIA, Cisco, TE, Amphenol | Extends the pluggable era. AAOI is not a named participant; Cisco's CEO puts it years out [T] |
| AEC / copper | Retimed copper for short links | Credo, cable primes | Takes in-rack and adjacent-rack links, limited by reach |
| MicroLED active light cable | Many slow optical channels | Credo, shipping planned 2027 | Attacks short optical links without lasers; vendor claims are unproven [T] |
| Captive design plus contract manufacturing | Customer owns the design | NVIDIA with Fabrinet and TFC | Removes the module vendor's margin altogether |

**Convergence read.** Near term the market is coexisting at 200G per lane, with mix moving toward CW-fed silicon photonics because InP is scarce. Longer term it points toward high-power CW lasers for NPO and CPO, with timing disputed: LightCounting, cited by Ciena, puts CPO overtaking pluggables at 200G+ per lane around 2031, while Yole sees large-scale deployment in 2028–2030 [T]. Reliable 1.6T silicon-photonics share data was not available.

**What the laser fab is worth today.** Owning lasers keeps AAOI out of the merchant EML queue, and the CEO puts laser gross margin above 60% [T]. Those are real benefits. But they did not prevent 1.6T from being supply-limited, because the binding parts are DSPs and related electronics, which AAOI buys. Silicon photonics also reduces the laser count and makes lower-power CW lasers easier to source. The fab's most valuable future use may be high-power CPO lasers, where AAOI has shown a 400mW DFB laser and says it is qualifying with four or five customers, but we found no named customer, third-party test or volume order [T].

**Defensibility.** AAOI held 199 US, 140 China/Taiwan and 10 European issued patents at FY25 [P]. It has been the plaintiff against Eoptolink, Accelight, CIG and Molex, but the asserted module patents are not evidence of a protected laser position, and PTAB instituted review on the '818 patent and a related petition in 2026 [T].

**Verdict: emerging (optionality), not yet durable.** The fab is proprietary know-how with a long replication time, and it could become more valuable if high-power lasers or US-supply rules develop as management hopes. Today it does not produce a visible margin premium: consolidated non-GAAP gross margin was 28.7% in Q3.

## 6. Secular / thematic positioning

AAOI has **second-derivative exposure to AI capex**. Spending reaches it only after accelerators, networks, and the customer's choice of optical suppliers. That exposure remains powerful. NVIDIA reported FQ3'27 data center revenue of $107.6B and guided total Q4 revenue to $130B ±3% [P]. Four large US cloud buyers point to roughly $730B of 2026 capex at guide midpoints, on tracker data [T3]. Amazon says it will spend meaningfully more in 2027, and Alphabet expects a significant increase [T3].

The change is **how that spending is financed**. Alphabet's Q3 free cash flow was −$9.1B and Amazon's trailing figure −$42.4B [T3]. CNBC, citing Goldman, reported $296B of hyperscaler debt issuance year to date and an expected $440B next year plus $300B at project level [T]. Demand can remain strong while the marginal dollar becomes more sensitive to credit markets.

CATV is a separate, **first-derivative but cyclical** exposure to DOCSIS 4.0 upgrades. Charter's actual upgrade/rebuild capex rose to $741M in Q3 from $484M, though Cox affects comparability [P]. Its total capex is guided from about $13.0B in 2026 to $11B or below in 2027, and management says network investment declines after next year [P/T]. That supports another strong year or so and an eventual fade. Harmonic's 64% revenue growth confirms the cycle is broad [T].

Policy is a potential third theme. A US-supply premium would be valuable, and the CFO said in March that customers accept 10–15% more for US production [T]. We found no independent proof of that premium, and AAOI's US manufacturing was only 0.6% of Q3 revenue.

## 8. Risks

**Pre-mortem: the single most likely fundamental path to a near-total equity loss.** AAOI commits to a large capacity build on the assumption that 1.6T and 800G ramp through 2027. DSP shortages and Texas power delays push revenue to the right, while vendors and equipment commitments come due. ## 12. Asymmetry verdict

**Floor.** There is no hard floor. Tangible book was $24.89 per share at 9/30, or $27.69 pro forma for the ATM at $45.16 [P], but much of it is construction, prepayments, inventory and receivables whose value depends on the ramp.

**Ceiling.** If AAOI converts orders and improves margins, the bull range is about 2.2–2.8x the current price. The stock traded above $200 only six months ago, but that price is not a useful anchor because the share count, margins and credit backdrop have changed.

**Verdict.** This is a **leveraged directional bet with fat tails**, not a clean low-floor, high-ceiling asymmetry. The upside is large and the expected value appears modestly positive on our subjective numbers, but dilution and financing can turn a good demand outcome into a poor per-share outcome.

## 15. Investment thesis (synthesis)

- **Demand is genuine and large.** Management reports about $450M of 1.6T orders and guides Q4 datacenter revenue up 74–93% QoQ. **Counterweight:** purchase orders can move, double ordering is a known sector risk, and a roughly $50M customer appears to have fallen sharply in Q3.
- **Long-term customer commitment has begun.** The agreement through May 2029 could bring $1.0–1.5B and a larger one is promised. **Counterweight:** the first deal is conditional, price-capped and capital-free, and the second is unsigned.
- **Capacity may become strategically valuable.** More than 1M units per month is targeted by end-2027, with a possible US policy tailwind. **Counterweight:** power is delaying Texas, current production is almost entirely in Taiwan and China, and funding is not yet secure.
- **Valuation has reset.** At about 2.1x annualized Q4 guided revenue, investors no longer pay the May multiple. **Counterweight:** low-priced issuance can transfer much of a recovery away from today's holders, and tangible book is not a hard floor.

**What has to be true:** order growth must become delivered revenue at improving gross margin before the company exhausts its practical supply of cheap vendor credit, covenant room and authorized shares.

## 18. Final thoughts & conclusion

AAOI has changed from a stock priced for scarcity into a company being tested on delivery. The business is larger and more diversified than it was in July: datacenter is now the majority of revenue, Digicomm's share of receivables is lower, and a first long-term contract exists. Yet the print exposed the weakness of the earlier narrative. Revenue will miss the old full-year frame, operating income will fall far short, margin is moving the wrong way, and owning lasers did not solve the 1.6T shortage. The 81.7% drawdown has removed much valuation risk, but it has also made equity funding more expensive and more dangerous.

**Edge — what do you believe that the crowd doesn't?**

> **The tape treats AAOI as a broken dilution story and the Street still models a franchise ramp; the variant view is that both are pricing the wrong variable. Demand is not the question. The question is whether a minority, price-capped supplier can earn per-share returns on a roughly $2B capacity build that depends on bought DSPs, delayed power, vendor credit and equity sold about 80% below its high.** AAOI can deliver very large revenue growth and still disappoint owners if price-downs, expedite costs and share issuance absorb the gain. The in-house laser fab is real optionality, especially for high-power lasers, but it is not yet shown to be the source of current economics; the binding 1.6T constraint is purchased DSP supply, and consolidated gross margin is falling as volume rises. CATV remains useful cash ballast, but it is shrinking toward about a quarter of revenue and no longer decides the equity case by itself. **AAOI would become a franchise only on evidence the current record lacks: customer capital or firm take-or-pay volume, non-GAAP gross margin sustained at 35% or above, operating cash flow positive before payables stretch, and disclosed high-margin external laser revenue.** Until then, consensus revenue of about $2.38B should not be capitalized as though it were durable per-share earnings power.

**Tripwires — observable events that would prove the current view wrong or require re-underwriting.** Pre-commit to re-underwrite or exit if any occurs.

- **(1) CATV channel breaks.** Any Digicomm receivable reserve or write-down; Digicomm receivable days, measured on that quarter's Digicomm revenue, rising back above 240 (6/30/26 level, about 239); or CATV revenue falling more than 30% QoQ.

- **(3) The reset ramp slips.** Q4'26 revenue below the $385M low end; cumulative 1.6T revenue below $200M through the Q1'27 report; or a Q1'27 revenue guide midpoint below Q4'26 actual revenue.
- **(4) Dilution becomes forced.** More than $200M of the fourth ATM disclosed as sold at an average below $60, or a stockholder request to raise authorized shares while ATM capacity remains unused because the limit is binding.
- **(5) Credit support breaks.** Any East West covenant breach, waiver or adverse amendment; revocation or non-renewal of the SPD line without replacement; or a decline of more than $100M in payables in a quarter while operating cash flow before working capital remains negative.
