# EQUITY RESEARCH — DEEP DIVE (EXHAUSTIVE DILIGENCE)

# COHR — Coherent Corp.

NYSE:COHR · no ADR · Saxonburg, Pennsylvania · FY ends June 30

*A capacity-constrained AI-optics compounder that has signed $11.8B of fixed supplier commitments against $63M of customer cash — sized for an indium-phosphide scarcity the architecture is designing out.*

---

## Executive summary — investment thesis

Coherent grows its own indium-phosphide and gallium-arsenide wafers, fabricates its own lasers, and assembles them into the 800G/1.6T optical transceivers that move data inside AI clusters; Datacenter & Communications was **79% of Q4 FY26 revenue and grew +58.6% YoY** **[P]**. **The debate is durability and price, not demand or solvency**: revenue is effectively sold out through 2027, but the FY2026 10-K discloses that Coherent financed that ramp itself — **free cash flow −$1,023.4M**, and **future purchase commitments up from $1.09B to $11.8B in twelve months** against **$63M of customer advances** — while the per-bit laser content it is scaling for is falling as silicon photonics and co-packaged optics take over.

**Three reasons to own, each with its counterweight.** **(1)** Demand is contracted and accelerating — Q4 D&C +58.6%, Q1 FY27 guided to $2.2–2.4B, 2027 capacity "effectively sold out," and NVIDIA put $2.0B of equity behind a supply agreement through 2030 — *but* that agreement is non-exclusive, its terms are unfiled, NVIDIA wrote the identical cheque to Lumentum, and NVIDIA's first production co-packaged-optics switch names **Lumentum and TFC, not Coherent**, as its laser suppliers **[P/T]**. **(2)** Coherent owns the scarce input — 6-inch InP running at yields above its 3-inch lines, capacity doubling in 2026 and more than doubling again in 2027 **[P]** — *but* every one of those lasers is consumed captively, and InP die per 1.6T port falls roughly **4x (≈8 EML → ≈2 CW)** as silicon photonics passes **50% of transceivers in 2026** while Broadcom triples and Lumentum adds 50%+ of capacity into the same window **[T]**. **(3)** The balance sheet absorbs a lot — net leverage ~0.73x, no convertibles, no ATM, nothing material maturing before 2029 **[P]** — *but* net debt fell this year only because of NVIDIA's $2.0B and $0.53B of asset sales; without them it rose ~$1.0B, and operating cash flow of $79.5M was itself held up by a **$748.5M stretch in payables** **[P]**.

**What has to be true:** InP stays scarce through 2028 despite falling die-per-bit and multiplying capacity, so the $11.8B of commitments reads as secured supply rather than fixed cost; Coherent converts its CPO "anchor customer" status at NVIDIA into disclosed production volume starting with the December 2026 quarter; and free cash flow turns positive in FY2027 without another equity injection.

**Bottom line.** At **$337.04 (2026-10-02)** — ~43x Q1-guided annualized non-GAAP EPS, ~41x trailing EV/EBITDA — the stock is priced for the consensus "sold-out compounder" outcome. The variant view is that the contracts bind Coherent far harder than they bind its customers. That asymmetry is invisible while InP is scarce and becomes the whole story the quarter it is not.

## 1. Business model & role in the value chain

Coherent is vertically integrated from substrate to module: it grows InP and GaAs wafers, fabricates EMLs, CW DFB lasers, VCSELs and photodiodes, and assembles transceivers, coherent modules, external-laser-source (ELS) modules and optical circuit switches. A legacy Industrial business (lasers, materials, SiC substrates) is being pruned by divestiture.

**What Coherent is not.** Not a merchant laser supplier: management said on 2026-08-12 *"I do not see any time in the near future where we would be selling Indium Phosphide lasers externally"* — every InP laser it makes is consumed inside its own modules **[P]**. Not a silicon-photonics foundry (that is TSMC, Tower, Intel/Jabil). Not a switch vendor — in co-packaged optics the optical engine belongs to NVIDIA/TSMC or Broadcom. Its position is the integrated module maker that owns its laser supply, which is a strength while lasers are the binding constraint and a cost while they are not.

## 4. Technological moat & competing approaches

| Approach | InP die per 1.6T | Champions | Trade-off vs Coherent's integrated EML/CW + module model |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EML pluggable** (Coherent's legacy strength) | **≈8 EML** | EML die: Lumentum, Broadcom, Mitsubishi (~72% combined); Coherent not top three **[T]** | Best reach and signal quality; consumes the most InP and the scarcest part (EML). The architecture being displaced at the margin |
| **SiPho pluggable + CW laser** | **≈2 CW** | Innolight, Eoptolink, Broadcom, Marvell, Jabil, Credo; Coherent also | ~4x fewer InP die, CMOS-wafer scale; value moves to the SiPho foundry and assembler. **>50% of a ~$40B transceiver market in 2026** (LightCounting) **[T]** |
| **CPO / NPO with external laser source** | **≈2 UHP CW** | NVIDIA Spectrum-X/Quantum-X (TSMC COUPE), Broadcom TH6-Davisson | **Deletes the pluggable module — Coherent's largest revenue line.** Fewer, higher-ASP lasers; engine value to TSMC and the switch vendor. NVIDIA claims **4x fewer lasers** |
| Heterogeneous InP-on-Si | integrated | NewPhotonics + Tower (volume shipping 2026-09-17); Intel via Jabil | Removes the discrete laser and attach step; if yield matures, bypasses both merchant and captive laser fabs |
| VCSEL / GaAs CPO | **0 InP** | Coherent (2.3 Tb/s 1060nm array, ECOC); Broadcom | Short-reach scale-up only. Coherent claims largest datacom VCSEL supplier — a hedge outside the InP fab |
| Copper / AEC | 0 | Credo (>$600M FY27 optical guide too) | Pushes the copper-to-optical crossover out at short reach |
| OCS | — | Google in-house (MEMS), Lumentum, Coherent (LCoS) | Google builds most of its own; ~5–6K of ~18K 2026 units bought outside **[T3]** |

**Convergence:** silicon photonics plus shared high-power InP CW lasers — pluggable now, NPO/CPO with external lasers from 2027, EML retained for the highest-performance lanes. Three drivers: EML scarcity (NVIDIA pre-allocated, lead times past 2027), a 4x cut in laser die per port, and CMOS-foundry scale. **LightCounting's framing is the one to hold: EMLs "may be replaced by silicon photonics," but the overall InP laser market keeps growing** **[T]** — which is true in units and silent on whether supply grows faster.

**Defensibility.** *Proprietary and defensible:* the 6-inch InP process (first in volume, yields above 3-inch across CW, EML and photodiode, "4x output at half cost"), and VCSEL scale. *Integration layer:* transceiver assembly — the business where Coherent is visibly losing share. *Patents:* not a barrier any rival has had to litigate around; no public disputes found. *Capital barrier:* real but being crossed — Broadcom, Lumentum (Greensboro fab revenue early 2028), and AXT's six-year capacity reservation with Lumentum.

**Verdict: technological moat EMERGING — durable only while InP stays supply-constrained.**

## 6. Secular / thematic positioning

Coherent is a **first-derivative** beneficiary of AI networking spend on revenue: every GPU cluster needs optical bandwidth, and the AI back-end network is "supply-constrained, rather than demand-constrained, for at least the next one-to-two years" (Dell'Oro) **[T]**. It is a **second-derivative** beneficiary on returns: those depend on laser content per bit, share, and the cost of the capacity it pre-commits to — and the first two are moving against it as the theme matures. **Exposure to AI optics volume is not exposure to InP scarcity rents**, and the stock is priced as if it were both.

## 8. Risks

### Pre-mortem — the most likely mechanism of permanent impairment

**Not a demand collapse and not insolvency. A fixed-cost trap built in a scarcity that ends.**

Through 2027 the industry does what it has said it will: Broadcom triples its InP fabs, Lumentum adds 50%+ and brings Greensboro on, Coherent doubles and doubles again, Tower and NewPhotonics ship heterogeneous InP-on-silicon at volume. Simultaneously the mix shifts — silicon photonics already passed half of transceivers in 2026, and the 1.6T ramp runs at ~2 CW lasers per port instead of ~8 EMLs. Laser *units* still grow; **laser supply grows faster**. Sometime in 2027–28, InP stops being the binding constraint.

At that point the contracts Coherent signed in FY2026 invert. **$8.4B of purchase commitments beyond FY2027**, with minimum-purchase requirements and liquidated-damages provisions, were sized for a world where every laser it could make was pre-sold. Its customer contracts are non-exclusive and unfiled, and its customers have put down **$63M**. Pricing weakens first in transceivers, where Coherent already trails Innolight and Eoptolink, and gross margin — 10 points below Lumentum's at the top of the cycle — compresses from an already-thin base. The **$2.6B of inventory, 60% of it WIP**, is valued for a mix that has moved. A write-down follows, then a goodwill test on a Lasers unit with ~8% headroom.

Because FCF never turned positive during the boom, there is no cash cushion built up: the ramp was funded by NVIDIA's equity, asset sales, $748.5M of supplier credit, and — by then — a drawn off-parent facility. **The killing detail:** the purchase commitments cannot be cut on the same timetable as customer orders. Revenue reprices in a quarter; the minimum purchases run on contract.

The equity does not go to zero — Coherent remains a scaled, profitable optics supplier with 0.7x leverage and no maturity before 2029. It de-rates from ~43x guided earnings to a mid-teens multiple on reset earnings: **roughly −50–60%**, and permanent, because the capacity and commitments will already exist.

**The tell:** purchase commitments disclosed above $11.8B while contract liabilities stay flat — Coherent committing faster than its customers do.

### Named risks

**Concentration.** One customer **20%** of FY26 revenue, a second **12%** — 32% from two accounts, up from 22% **[P]**. Both must be North America-headquartered (each exceeds every non-NA region's revenue). The 20% customer is **most plausibly NVIDIA, moderate-to-high confidence, not established** — the filing never links them. **Filing inconsistency:** Note 20's text says the 20% customer was 10% in FY25 and FY24; the XBRL tags it at 12% in FY25 (caveat 4).

**Counterparty (NVIDIA).** Non-exclusive, terms unfiled, identical deal with Lumentum, and NVIDIA's first production CPO names Lumentum's lasers. NVIDIA itself is strong (Q2 FY27 revenue $96.2B, H1 OCF $74.4B) but has taken on new obligations — **$24.9B net new debt in H1** and **residual-value guaranties capped at $105B** tied to OpenAI's SB Energy campus **[P]**.

**End-buyer financing.** Hyperscaler capex is now partly debt-funded (§3 macro). Demand is intact; its financing has become rate-sensitive at exactly the moment rates turned.

**Supply.** AXT (6-inch InP substrates, Beijing) names China's InP export permits "the most significant challenge we currently face" **[P]**. China's Ga/Ge/Sb suspension ends ~2026-11-27. Germanium, yttrium and InP are named 10-K risks.

**Regulatory.** A BIS inquiry into past Huawei sales, open since January 2025, with no loss estimate possible **[P]**. Tariff and Section 232 exposure; the 2/20/26 IEEPA ruling and refund uncertainty.

**Accounting.** Goodwill headroom ~8% on $3.1B (CAM). The equity statement labels the Series B conversion "Series A" — a filing erratum, immaterial but sloppy.

**Disclosure gaps.** No backlog or RPO; NVIDIA agreements not filed; no FY27 cash or capex guidance; the off-parent facility disclosed only in a 10-K note.

## 10. Sector bottleneck / structural analysis

**The binding constraint in AI optics is laser die — and it is the constraint every participant is now spending to remove.** Innolight, Eoptolink and T&S all named 200G EML chips as the cap on 1.6T delivery in July; Lumentum calls itself "effectively sold out for the foreseeable future"; Broadcom says laser demand is "fast surpassing supply"; Coherent says InP capacity is its "primary constraint" **[P/T]**.

Coherent's position on that constraint is ambiguous in a way the July report understated. It **owns** InP supply, which is why it can ship 1.6T while module assemblers queue for die. But it **sells none of it** — so it does not monetize the scarcity as a merchant (Lumentum and Broadcom do), and it is itself net *short* InP: management expects "some portion" of future datacom transceivers to be "supported by external sources." The scarcity protects Coherent's module share; it does not pay Coherent a rent.

The constraint is also eroding from two directions at once: demand-side (fewer die per bit) and supply-side (capacity multiplying, heterogeneous integration shipping). **A bottleneck that participants are attacking from both ends rarely survives a full capex cycle**, and Coherent's FY2026 contracts commit it to that cycle's peak volumes through 2030.

## 15. Investment thesis (synthesis)

**1 — Contracted, accelerating AI-optics demand.** D&C +58.6%, Q1 guided +12% QoQ, 2027 sold out, NVIDIA $2.0B behind it. *Counterweight:* the customer-side contracts are soft — non-exclusive, unfiled, $63M of advances — and NVIDIA's first production CPO buys its lasers from Lumentum.

**What has to be true:** InP scarcity outlasts the 2027–28 capacity wave, Coherent converts NVIDIA anchor status into disclosed CPO volume, and FCF turns positive in FY27 without new equity. **Three conditions, each contested by dated evidence.**

## 18. Final thoughts & conclusion

Coherent executed FY2026 exactly as a capacity-constrained supplier should: it beat every guided line, doubled InP capacity a quarter early, deleveraged and simplified its capital structure. The sell-side is unanimous and the order book runs into 2028. **And it paid for all of it with its own balance sheet and its suppliers' patience — FCF −$1.02B, payables +$748.5M, $11.8B of commitments — while its customers committed $63M of cash and its largest customer's first production CPO switch shipped with a competitor's lasers.** The July thesis was built on the wrong half of that picture.

### Edge — what do you believe that the crowd doesn't?

> **The market reads Coherent's contracts as a demand signal. They are equally a fixed cost — and they bind Coherent far harder than they bind its customers.** The consensus case is a capacity-constrained AI-optics compounder: 2027 sold out, orders into 2028, LTAs to the end of the decade, NVIDIA's $2.0B behind it, 23–24 analysts at Buy. All true. What the consensus does not model is the other side of those agreements, which the FY2026 10-K put on the record. **Future purchase commitments rose from $1.09B to $11.8B in twelve months — $8.4B of it beyond FY2027, from $147M a year earlier — with minimum-purchase requirements and liquidated-damages provisions.** Against that, **customers have advanced $63M**, down year on year; the NVIDIA agreement is non-exclusive and unfiled; and NVIDIA's $2.0B arrived as equity, which binds NVIDIA to nothing. Coherent's own MD&A says the NVIDIA capacity agreement "may require incremental investments in equipment, labor, and working capital… through 2030."
>
> **The differentiated call is that those commitments are sized for an indium-phosphide scarcity the architecture is designing out.** Laser die per 1.6T port falls roughly 4x — about 8 EMLs in a pluggable to about 2 CW lasers in silicon photonics or co-packaged optics — and silicon photonics passed half of all transceivers in 2026. In co-packaged optics the pluggable module, Coherent's largest revenue line, disappears. At the same time industry InP supply is multiplying: Broadcom more than tripling, Lumentum adding 50%+, Coherent itself doubling twice, heterogeneous InP-on-silicon shipping at volume. Coherent sells none of its InP externally, so the scarcity protects its module share but pays it no rent — and NVIDIA's first production CPO switch names Lumentum, not Coherent, as its laser supplier. While InP is scarce, $11.8B of secured supply is an advantage. The quarter it is not, it is a liability that does not reprice with demand.
>
> **The cash statement already shows the cost, and the published models still do not.** FY2026 free cash flow was −$1,023.4M and worsened every quarter; operating cash flow of $79.5M was propped by a $748.5M stretch in payables (≈ −$669M without it); net debt fell only because of NVIDIA's equity and asset sales. Not one of ~10 dated sell-side rationales since the print mentions cash, inventory or working capital, and the board's $100M PSU grant attaches zero weight to any of them.
>
> **What this Edge explicitly does not claim.** It does not claim AI optics demand fails — hyperscaler capex is still rising. It does not claim Coherent is distressed — leverage is ~0.73x and nothing material matures before 2029. It does not claim the commitments are wrong — if InP stays scarce through 2028 they are exactly right. And it does not claim the market has ignored the cash — **COHR has underperformed Lumentum by ~23 points since the print**, so the tape has priced some of it. **It claims the asymmetry between what Coherent has committed to and what its customers have committed to is unmodeled, and that its sign flips with InP scarcity, which is the variable the industry is spending to remove.**

### Tripwires — the falsification triggers and committed actions

- **(1) Cash conversion — the forward test.** On the Q2 FY27 report (~early Feb 2027), if **cumulative H1 FY27 free cash flow is worse than −$500M** (H2 FY26 was −$869.8M), OR **inventory exceeds ~$3.0B at 12/31/26**, the ramp is consuming capital faster than it converts and the commitments are being carried on the balance sheet. **Decide before the print.**
- **(2) The contractual asymmetry widens.** Any disclosure of total future purchase commitments **above $11.8B while contract liabilities remain below ~$150M**, OR **any draw on the ~$945M off-parent working-capital facility** — Coherent committing faster than its customers, or the build moving onto unguaranteed subsidiary debt.
- **(3) Demand digestion at the source.** Any of Microsoft / Alphabet / Meta / Amazon **cutting or materially softening** 2026–2027 capex guidance.
- **(4) The CPO laser socket goes elsewhere.** **(a)** Coherent's December 2026-quarter CPO / UHP CW laser revenue fails to begin as guided, OR NVIDIA or Broadcom discloses an external-laser supplier split with Coherent below roughly one-third; **(b)** a third and fourth vendor (beyond Lumentum and Coherent) **shipping production volume** into the OIF-ELSFP / external-laser-source socket; **(c)** NVIDIA's 13F shows a **reduction of its 7,788,161-share COHR position** while its Lumentum position holds. This remains the only trigger that permanently impairs terminal margin.
- **(5) Share loss accelerates.** Two consecutive LightCounting/Cignal AI vendor rankings showing Coherent's high-speed datacom share falling, or D&C revenue growth decelerating below ~25% YoY while Innolight/Eoptolink continue compounding faster.
- **(6) Upward falsifier — re-underwrite, do not exit.** **Contract liabilities rise above ~$500M** (customers put cash behind the commitments), OR **free cash flow is positive for two consecutive quarters with inventory declining** — the asymmetry has closed and the commitments were pre-sold supply. **Committed action: re-underwrite upward.**

### Time-horizon fit

The thesis resolves over **FY2027 (through ~Aug 2027)**: Q1 and Q2 cash statements (Nov 2026, Feb 2027), the December-quarter CPO revenue start, and the FY27 10-K's commitment and contract-liability disclosures. The structural half — InP scarcity ending — plays out over **2027–28**. A horizon shorter than two quarters is mostly trading the optical basket (R² 0.85).
