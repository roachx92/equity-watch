## Executive summary — investment thesis

**What this is.** Cipher Digital is a former bitcoin miner that has become a **developer-lessor of AI/HPC data centers on owned ERCOT (Texas) grid capacity**. It builds campuses and leases them, powered-shell-plus-operations, on 15–20 year triple-net terms. Three campuses are leased: **Black Pearl** (216 critical-IT MW, 15 years, ~$5.5B, tenant Amazon Data Services with a **full Amazon.com parent guarantee of base rent and opex**; first capacity delivered early August 2026 and **rent has commenced**), **Barber Lake** (207 critical-IT MW, tenant **Fluidstack**, with a Google credit arrangement, amended 2026-09-24 to a 20-year aggregate term and **over $9B**), and **Stingray** (70 critical-IT MW, 15 years, ~$2.0B, investment-grade hyperscaler, delivery from 2027). Total contracted ~**$16.5B on 493 critical-IT MW** **[P]**. Bitcoin mining is being deliberately run down and is still **100% of recognized revenue** — Q2 2026 revenue was **$24.8M, down 43% year over year**, against a **$267.5M quarterly net loss** and **negative $30M of adjusted EBITDA** **[P]**.

**The debate is not whether the pivot is real, and it is not solvency. It is whether the equity — a $562.1M sliver of book value under $6.0155B of gross debt, with 49.3% of the share count invisible to every screen — captures the value of a contracted book the market already believes in.**

**Three hard, primary-sourced anchor facts:**

1. **The fully diluted share count is 619,733,668 — 49.3% above the 415,030,722 every aggregator quotes.** Note-conversion dilution alone is **161,930,746 shares**, up **3.2x in twelve months**, and both convertible series are **in the money** at $17.73 (2030s convert at **$4.4460**, 2031s at **$16.0325**) **[P]**. On the diluted count Cipher trades at **$22.3M of equity value per contracted critical-IT MW** against Hut 8 at **$12.6M** on the same construction — it is the *most* expensive name in its cohort per megawatt and per dollar of backlog, not the cheapest **[P for inputs, derived]**.
2. **The Google "backstop" is not a rent guarantee.** EX-10.1 (A&R Recognition Agreement) provides that on a Fluidstack default Google may pay an **"FS Termination Fee"** which is **capped and declines over time on a schedule filed as Schedule C — and Schedule C is redacted**; the money is paid **"directly into the Designated FS Termination Fee Account"** held under an agreement among Google, Alphabet, the Landlord and **Wilmington Trust as notes trustee**; and **upon payment "Google will be provided an equity interest position in Landlord."** Google's liability "**IS LIMITED TO THE AMOUNT OF THE FS TERMINATION FEE**." The company's own footnote states the **"Cipher Compute LLC high-yield bond amortizes in line with the Google backstop"** **[P]**. The wrapper protects the **lenders** and **dilutes Cipher's project equity** when it fires.
3. **1H 2026 consumed $152.0M of operating cash while spending $964.3M of cash capex — approximately $1,431M on an accrual basis** — funded by **$2,766.4M of note proceeds**. Cash rose from $628.3M to $831.8M entirely on financing. **Construction in progress is $1,676.1M of $2,368.1M of gross PP&E — 70.8% of the asset base is not in service, not depreciating and not earning** **[P]**.

**Three reasons to own — each with its honest counterweight, developed here and not deferred:**

**(1) Cipher is the only company in its cohort actually collecting hyperscale HPC rent, and it is roughly 9–18 months ahead on delivered megawatts.** Black Pearl's first capacity landed **early August 2026, two months ahead of schedule at the tenant's request**, with rent commenced **[P]**. Peer first deliveries: Hut 8 River Bend Q2 2027 and Beacon Point Q3 2027; Riot December 2027; TeraWulf/Anthropic H2 2027–early 2028; Applied Digital H1 2028; Core Scientific/AMD H1 2027 **[P/T]**. In a market where JLL puts North American colocation vacancy at **1% for a third year** and CBRE records **1.4%** in H1 2026 with 80.4% of 7,481 MW under construction pre-committed **[T]**, delivering now is worth a great deal. *Counterweight:* it is a **lead measured in quarters, not a barrier**. And "Black Pearl rent commenced" overstates it — the Q2 update shows rent commenced on *first* capacity while **remaining Phase I halls were in MEP fit-out and Phase II at concrete foundations** **[P]**. A fraction of 216 MW is paying.

**(2) The contracted book carries genuinely excellent credit at the top, and the financing is pre-placed campus by campus.** Amazon.com guarantees base rent *and* operating expenses on ~$7.5B — roughly 45% of the book — and Amazon covers overruns above $9.5M per IT MW at Black Pearl **[P]**. Each campus is financed in its own SPV against its own lease, and the coupon *fell* across three successive deals (7.125% → 6.125% → 6.000%), with the Stingray issue explicitly funding development "**through substantial completion**" and reimbursing $56.7M of spent capex **[P]**. The credit market has been re-rating the structure upward while the equity de-rated. *Counterweight:* **all of it matures 2031** against 15–20 year leases. Hut 8 funds identical assets with **$7.5B of investment-grade paper (Baa2 / BBB−) at 6.129–6.192% due 2042** plus a $1.07B JPMorgan-led revolver **[P/T]**. Same coupon, investment grade, eleven more years of tenor. Cipher's terminal value depends on refinancing ~$4.5B of high-yield bullets in 2031.

**(3) Rent has started, the tenant base is expanding rather than retreating, and the land-and-power bank is repricing.** Fluidstack has added capacity twice (+39 MW in November 2025; the 20-year restructuring in September 2026) and has "**commenced beneficial use… including partial occupancy of the building and deployment of network racks**" **[P]** — a tenant installing racks is not a tenant looking for an exit. ERCOT conditionally classified **3.2 GW** of Cipher projects, and the stock closed **+10.80%** the session the market learned it **[P for classification, settled bar for the move]**. *Counterweight:* **contracted critical-IT MW has not moved — 493 MW in July, 493 MW today.** The entire +$5.2B came from *extending term on megawatts already contracted*, not from new capacity, while Hut 8 ($26.6B / 949 MW) and TeraWulf (one $19B Anthropic lease) grew on volume **[P/T]**. And **66% of the 3.2 GW landed in the weaker Studied Load tier** — the worst tier mix in the cohort against IREN, Ionic and Hut 8 at 100% Base Load **[T for the split]**.

**What has to be true.** All four, together: (i) the per-hall Barber Lake commencements land through Q4 2026–Q1 2027 **without triggering the rent credits the 10-Q contemplates**; (ii) capex peaks and operating cash flow inflects before the 2031 maturity wall becomes a refinancing problem; (iii) ERCOT's **spring 2027** final allocation does not haircut the 2.1 GW Studied Load pipeline that is the entire growth leg; and (iv) the market eventually pays for contracted backlog again — which it has conspicuously stopped doing, as Hut 8's **11% fall on a $19.6B backlog print (2026-08-21)** and Cipher's own **−2.04% on a >$9B print (2026-09-25)** both demonstrate **[settled bars / T]**.

**Bottom line.** The franchise is real, the execution record is the best in the cohort, and the top of the credit stack is excellent. But the equity is a thin, heavily diluted claim on a levered construction project whose headline backlog is worth less per megawatt than any peer's, whose credit wrapper pays its lenders first, and whose growth leg sits in the tier ERCOT has not yet allocated. **Fairly valued at best on the diluted count; the risk is not that the thesis fails but that it succeeds and the equity does not capture it.**

## 1. Business model & role in the value chain

Cipher makes money in exactly one way today and intends to make it in another way tomorrow. Today: **selling self-mined bitcoin**. Tomorrow: **collecting triple-net rent on data-center shells it builds on power it already controls.** The transition is mid-flight and the financial statements show only the first half of it.

| Unallocated (comp & benefits, G&A) | — | — | — | — | $(58,838)K |

*Source: 10-Q Note 19 **[P]**. Also within the segment result: change in fair value of the power purchase agreement $(5,900)K, unrealized gain on bitcoin $16,901K, realized loss on bitcoin sales $(23,509)K, other $2,384K.*

**There is no HPC segment yet.** 10-Q Note 19, verbatim: *"The Company currently has one operating segment, Bitcoin Mining… In future periods, the Company expects to have an additional segment related to construction of data centers for HPC tenants when leases for those data centers commence operations"* **[P]**. And Note 12: *"These leases have not commenced and no revenue has been recognized as of June 30, 2026."* Black Pearl's rent commenced in **August** — after the balance-sheet date — so it appears in the 10-Q only as a subsequent event (Note 21). **The first dollar of HPC revenue will appear in the Q3 2026 10-Q, which is unfiled.**

Revenue is falling because mining is being dismantled on purpose: miners held for sale went 94,879 → 0 and gross miner cost 289,680 → 185,676, and D&A **fell** from $87,554K to $38,379K over 1H **[P]**. That decline flatters the operating loss and should not be read as efficiency.

**Position in the value chain — and what Cipher is not.** It is **not** a cloud or a neocloud (it sells shells, not GPU-hours); **not** a GPU owner (tenants bring their own silicon, which is why the "$6.5B of GPU financing" circulating in secondary commentary does not exist — see caveat 9); **not** a utility or generator (it buys interconnection and power); and **not** a REIT. It sits between the **grid** and the **compute buyer**: land + an ERCOT interconnection position + construction management + project finance + a tenant relationship. Every input except the interconnection position is purchasable by anyone with capital — which is the moat question in §4–§5.

The equity story is the three leases and the ~4.2 GW pipeline behind them. Mining is a wasting funding bridge.

5. **ERCOT spring 2027 allocation** — how much of the 2.1 GW Studied Load tier survives?

### Net re-rate verdict

**The fundamental bucket carries the most weight for the next twelve months, and it is genuinely two-sided for the first time in this name's coverage** — a clean per-hall delivery sequence plus an OCF inflection is worth +35–50%, while a rent credit or a disclosed slip is worth −25–35%. The sentiment/technical bucket is unusually informative right now (IV in its top decile against calming realized vol, 21% short float with real days-to-cover, and no dedicated AI-datacenter bid), but it is transient by construction.

**Over 3–5 years the dominant bucket is structural, and it is not the one the market is watching: the 2031 refinancing into a post-ERCOT-allocation market.** Cipher must roll ~$4.5B of high-yield bullets against 15–20 year leases at precisely the moment its interconnection advantage may have been administratively competed away — while its closest comparable already holds 2042 investment-grade paper at the same coupon. That is a different question from anything resolving this quarter, and conflating the two is the most common error in the bull case.

## 4. Technological moat & competing approaches

### Level 1 — competing approaches to delivering AI compute capacity

Not chips. For a developer-lessor the rival "technologies" are siting strategies and capital structures.

| Approach | How it works | Champions | Trade-off vs Cipher's approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Converted mining sites with existing interconnect** *(Cipher's model)* | Own land already energized under a large-load interconnect from mining; retrofit for liquid cooling; lease as powered shell + ops | Cipher, Hut 8, TeraWulf, Core Scientific, Riot, IREN, Galaxy, Applied Digital, Bitdeer | **Fastest time-to-power in 2026–27** — Cipher delivered Black Pearl Phase I two months early **[P]**. But retrofits cap density, sites sit in weak-transmission ERCOT Far West, and scale-up needs *new* interconnects that queue like everyone else's |
| **(b) Traditional wholesale colocation developers** | Greenfield/brownfield campuses, decades of utility relationships, IG balance sheets, REIT/infra-fund equity | QTS/Blackstone (**>$150B portfolio + $160B pipeline**), Vantage, Aligned, STACK/Blue Owl, CloudHQ, Switch, Digital Realty **[T]** | **Vastly cheaper capital and a deeper execution bench**; slower on any *new* site. Trade-off: fuel/emissions, turbine lead times, no grid arbitrage |
| **(f) International / low-cost-power siting** | Cheap hydro/renewable power outside US queues | Bitdeer (Tydal, Norway), Nebius (**>1 GW by YE26 across four jurisdictions**) **[T]** | Bitdeer's Norway lease clears the **highest rent in the cohort (~$202/kW-month disclosed)** **[P]** — low-cost power does not require accepting low rent. Trade-off: FX, sovereign risk, latency |
| **(g) Longer-duration structural substitute: compute efficiency** | Better silicon and better software reduce megawatts per unit of intelligence | Nvidia/AMD roadmaps, inference optimization | The slow, unhedgeable risk to every power-scarcity thesis. Not a 2027 factor; a 2030s factor |

**Where the industry is converging: on (a) + (c) simultaneously, with (e) as the release valve.** Hyperscalers self-build the core and lease the marginal, time-critical megawatt to whoever has power *now* — which is why the ex-miner cohort won >$100B of contracts in eighteen months. But convergence is on **the power**, not the model. The scarcity *is* the market, and it is being competed away: **>66 GW under construction across North America, 77% in frontier markets** **[T]**.

### Defensibility assessment

**Proprietary defensible technology: none.** Cipher owns no proprietary technology, no patents of consequence, and no process advantage. This is an **integration layer** — land, an interconnection position, construction management, project finance, a tenant relationship. Every input except the interconnection position is purchasable with capital, and the interconnection position is precisely what a regulator is converting into an administratively rationed commodity.

**Switching costs are real but one-directional.** A tenant that has installed racks and network gear in a hall will not move mid-term — Fluidstack's beneficial use is genuine lock-in. But that lock-in begins only *after* delivery, which is the window in which all the risk sits. Before commencement the tenant holds termination rights and, per the Q2 10-Q, **rent-credit remedies**.

**Capital barrier: moderate, and pointed the wrong way.** Building a 200 MW campus takes ~$2B, which excludes most entrants — but *not* Blackstone, Blue Owl or the hyperscalers, and the cost of that capital is where Cipher loses to Hut 8 on identical assets.

**Verdict: technological moat ABSENT; commercial moat THIN-TO-MODERATE and EMERGING, with a dated expiry.** What is defensible is a **2026–27 delivery lead measured in quarters**, plus three energized campuses that were never subject to Batch Zero. That is a real, currently-unmatched position. It is an option, not a moat.

## 6. Secular / thematic positioning

Cipher is a **first-derivative** beneficiary of AI datacenter buildout on *contracted revenue* and a **third-derivative** beneficiary on *equity value* — and the distance between those is this report's subject.

**First derivative:** every incremental GPU cluster needs powered shell, and Cipher has 493 contracted critical-IT MW with rent starting. Vacancy at 1–1.4% and $844B of 2026 hyperscaler capex are genuine tailwinds **[T]**.

**Second derivative:** the *rent* depends on scarcity of interconnection, which is being administratively rationed away on a published calendar (December 2026 designations, spring 2027 allocations), and on bargaining power, which the $359.3M absorption suggests Cipher does not have much of.

**Third derivative — where the equity actually lives:** contracted rent must survive delivery (rent credits), clear ~$6.0B of debt service, and be divided by a share count 49.3% larger than the screens show. **Exposure to the theme is not capture of the theme**, and the gap here is unusually wide.

A genuine second theme is emerging and is *not* in the price: Cipher's ~4.2 GW land-and-power bank as an option independent of any lease. But 66% of the classified portion is in the Studied Load tier, so that option's strike is set in spring 2027.

## 8. Risks

### The pre-mortem — the single most likely mechanism by which this equity is permanently impaired

**Not bankruptcy, and not demand.** The realistic catastrophic path is a **rent-credit-and-refinancing squeeze that transfers the value of a working contracted book from equity to creditors and to Google**, and it runs like this:

Barber Lake's halls are delivered late — not catastrophically, just past their contractual milestones, which is exactly what the 9/24 amendment's "change orders and continued evolution of tenant requirements" language makes likely. The Q2 10-Q already discloses that Cipher "**may be subject to rent credits or other remedies for failure to achieve specified delivery milestones**" at both Black Pearl and Stingray **[P]**. Rent credits are **permanent revenue reductions, not deferrals** — they lower the contracted number itself. Simultaneously the **$359.3M first-loss overrun tranche** is consumed (the total Barber Lake budget is undisclosed, so nobody outside can see how close it is), and **amortization begins** on the Cipher Compute notes at ~$94.5M/year while rent is still arriving hall by hall.

Cipher funds the gap the only ways available: the **ATM** (at a price far below the ~$23.50 it realized in 1H26) and the **revolver** — whose draw is conditioned on a **minimum market capitalization** and whose maintenance covenant *tightens* to $150M then $200M of liquidity exactly as the facilities commence **[P]**. Each equity issuance lands on a base already carrying a **49.3% overhang** whose convertible component is in the money and will convert.

Then the terminal blow, which is structural rather than operational: **ERCOT's spring 2027 allocation** haircuts the 2.1 GW Studied Load tier — Apollo, Mikeska, McLennan, Stingray Phase II — removing both the growth leg and the scarcity premium underpinning the rent curve. Into that softer market Cipher must refinance **~$4.5B of 2031 secured bullets** against leases running to 2041+, while Hut 8 sits on 2042 investment-grade paper at the same coupon. Refinancing happens, but wider and with covenants; and if Fluidstack ever defaults, the Google fee retires notes and **hands Google an equity interest in Cipher Barber Lake LLC**, diluting the project at the asset level.

The equity does not go to zero: Amazon keeps paying on ~45% of the book and the assets are real. It de-rates from a growth multiple to a levered-yield multiple on a share count half again as large — **which from $17.73 is a decline of roughly 60–75%, and it is permanent because the capital will already have been spent and the shares already issued.**

**The tell that this is beginning:** any disclosure of a **rent credit** or delivery-milestone remedy in the Q3 or Q4 2026 10-Q. That single line item is the leading indicator, and no current tripwire watches it.

### Named and specific risks

**Concentration.** Two of three principal obligors — **~54% of contracted revenue** — are unrated (Fluidstack) or **unnamed** (the AI lab). Amazon is ~45% on one obligor. **Cipher discloses no customer-concentration percentages at all** — there is no >10%-customer note in the 10-Q or 10-K, because no HPC revenue had been recognized; the mining pool operator that is de facto ~100% of revenue is also unnamed **[P, by absence]**. Every campus is a **single-tenant property**, which the 10-K names as a risk.

**Interconnection and third parties, named in the filings.** The 10-Q's newly added risk factors name **Oncor Electric Delivery, American Electric Power and ERCOT** explicitly: *"our operations require approval to operate from Oncor, AEP and/or ERCOT, which can be onerous to obtain."* Also newly added: dependence on third parties for **substation construction**, expressly linked to "delay of rent commencement and potential breach and termination of our leases"; **West Texas labor** competition tied to "our actual costs exceeding our budget estimate"; and **community opposition**, which names "growing public skepticism and resistance to AI" as a source and contemplates being "forced to abandon the project" **[P]**.

**The guarantee asymmetry, in the company's own words.** *"Our tenants' guarantees and other backstop arrangements under our HPC leases **will only be effective after rent commencement** under such leases and are subject to certain limitations, like event of default triggers or caps."* The 10-K expands: *"If completion of the data centers are delayed, our tenants may have the right to terminate their lease. Such termination events **would not trigger the tenants' guarantee either**"* **[P]**.

**The backstop's actual mechanics (EX-10.1).** Capped and **declining** on a **redacted** schedule; paid to a **trustee-controlled account**; converts into **Google equity in the Landlord**; Google's alternative "Assumption Option" novates it in at **"Modified Rent Rates… (a 'Discounted Rent')"** with an early termination right (Schedule D, also redacted); and §12.b **automatically terminates the agreement on expiration of the Fluidstack Lease** — so years 11–20 are unsupported **[P]**.

**Leverage.** Gross principal **$6,015,500K** against **stockholders' equity of $562,057K** — roughly **10.7x** — with equity down from $805.5M (12/31/25) and $714.2M (3/31/26), i.e. **eroding ~$150M/quarter**, and an accumulated deficit of $1,385,501K. The **warrant liability alone ($632,060K) exceeds book equity [P]**.

**Financial-market dependence.** The revolver's borrowing condition includes a **minimum market capitalization**, and its liquidity covenant steps up as facilities commence — an unusual equity-market-dependent structure that tightens precisely when construction risk peaks **[P]**.

**Regulatory / geographic.** Texas concentration; ERCOT **curtailment obligations**, voltage and frequency ride-through; the **SB6 curtailment framework**; severe West Texas weather; and the conditional, revocable nature of Batch Zero classifications with removal possible for projects failing verification **[P]**.

**Insurance — and a notable absence.** The 10-K's Insurance section names property, builders' risk, commercial general liability and business interruption, but discloses **no limits, retentions, carriers or per-campus sublimits**, and does not say whether builders' risk covers **delay-in-startup or loss of rents** — which is the exposure that matters for a company with three concurrent multi-hundred-million-dollar builds (caveat 4). Two explicit negatives: **no cybersecurity insurance for digital assets** and **no insurance on bitcoin holdings** **[P]**.

**A risk factor that does not exist.** There is **no Item 1A risk factor for the Google warrant or the Top-Up Threshold** anywhere in the 10-K or 10-Q — despite a $632.1M liability and a potential cash-or-share obligation. It is disclosed only in Notes 16/18 **[P, by absence]**.

## 10. Sector bottleneck / structural analysis

**The binding constraint in AI infrastructure today is grid interconnection, and Cipher sits directly on it — for about eighteen more months.**

The evidence that it is binding is unambiguous: JLL puts North American colocation vacancy at **1% for a third consecutive year**; CBRE records a record-low **1.4%** in primary markets in H1 2026 with **7,481 MW under construction, 80.4% pre-committed**; and **>66 GW is under construction across North America, 77% in frontier markets** **[T]**. Tenants are contracting 2028 deliveries. The ERCOT queue exceeds **474 GW, ~90% data centers — more than five times Texas' record peak demand** **[T]**.

**The evidence that it is being liquidated is equally unambiguous, and it is on a published calendar.** ERCOT's Batch Zero sorted ~250–300 projects / ~200 GW, with the September 2026 board update indicating **Base Load 66.4 GW across 204 projects and Studied Load 125.4 GW across 158 projects** **[T — the primary PDF would not parse; treat the split as secondary]**. Conditional classifications issued 2026-09-03 under a PUCT good-cause exception granted 2026-08-20; three-business-day dispute windows; verification RFIs from 2026-09-09; **final designations December 2026 and final MW allocations spring 2027**, at which point a Studied Load project "may receive its full requested capacity, a partial allocation, or may need to wait until a subsequent batch" **[P]**.

**If the base-load tier alone is 66 GW, an existing interconnect does not confer scarcity past 2027.** It confers scarcity *right now*, in the 2026–27 window — which is exactly where Cipher's contracted book sits, and exactly where it ends.

**The second-order bottleneck is more durable and less discussed: the cost and tenor of capital for a levered lessor.** Once interconnection stops being the constraint, the game becomes who can fund a $2B campus most cheaply for longest. On that axis Hut 8 has already won and Cipher has already lost, on identical assets, at the same coupon.

**The third constraint is the one the queue-bypass players are monetizing:** if Crusoe/Homer City can deliver 4.5 GW behind the meter from 2027 and Bitdeer can book the cohort's highest rent in Norway, then **scarce Texas power is not a scarce input globally** — it is a scarce input locally, temporarily.

## 12. Asymmetry verdict

**Floor:** supported but distant. **$5–10.** Amazon.com guarantees ~45% of the contracted book; nothing matures before 2030; $3.73B of restricted cash pre-funds the build; and the assets are real, energized and in a market at 1% vacancy. Insolvency is not the realistic bear case. But **$6.0155B of gross debt against $562.1M of eroding book equity** means the equity is the thin residual, and small changes in stabilized EBITDA or share count move it violently.

**Ceiling:** capped by consensus, not by imagination. **$30–45.** The bull outcome is worth roughly where the sell-side average already sits ($31.13), because the market is *already* underwriting the contracted book — the analysts and the company agree about the future; only the tape dissents. There is no scenario in which Cipher is discovered.

**Verdict: this is NOT a genuine low-floor/high-ceiling asymmetric bet. It is a leveraged directional bet on construction execution with fat tails, dressed as a contracted-backlog story.**

Three reasons, in order:

1. **The payoff is not asymmetric — the *risk* is.** Downside −60–75%, upside +70–150%, but the downside resolves on a **single line item in one quarterly filing** (a rent credit) while the upside needs four sequential things over four to six quarters *plus* a market that starts paying for backlog again.
2. **The bet is not diversified across catalysts, despite appearing to be.** Barber Lake commencement, warrant Exercise Period, Cipher Compute amortization start, and the revolver's liquidity step-up **all key off the same undated Q4 2026 event**. Four mechanisms, one trigger.
3. **The share count is the hidden leg.** A 49.3% overhang with in-the-money converts means the equity you buy is not the equity you own. On the diluted count Cipher is the *richest* name in its cohort per contracted megawatt ($22.3M vs Hut 8's $12.6M) — so you are paying a premium for the most refinancing-dependent balance sheet in the group.

The genuinely asymmetric elements are smaller and optional: the ~4.2 GW land-and-power bank as an option independent of any lease (strike set in spring 2027), and a potential re-rating if a dedicated AI-infrastructure bid ever replaces the bitcoin-miner ETF as the marginal holder. Neither is large enough to carry the position.

## 15. Investment thesis (synthesis)

**Reason 1 — Cipher is the only company in its cohort collecting hyperscale HPC rent, roughly 9–18 months ahead, with the best counterparty in the group.** Black Pearl delivered two months early at the tenant's request; Amazon.com guarantees base rent *and* opex on ~45% of the contracted book and covers overruns above $9.5M/IT MW **[P]**. In a market at 1–1.4% vacancy, delivering now is worth a great deal.
*Counterweight:* the lead is measured in **quarters, not structural barriers**, and "rent commenced" describes a fraction of 216 MW while the remaining Phase I halls were in MEP fit-out and Phase II at foundations. Meanwhile **contracted critical-IT MW has been flat at 493 since July** — the entire +$5.2B came from duration, not capacity, while Hut 8 and TeraWulf grew on volume.

**Reason 2 — the contracted book is real, pre-funded campus by campus at falling coupons, and insulated from the ERCOT process that is the live overhang on peers.** Three SPV financings at 7.125% → 6.125% → 6.000%, $3.73B of restricted cash pre-funding the build, and **Barber Lake, Black Pearl and Odessa were never Batch Zero applicants** — so, unlike Hut 8's $19.6B Beacon Point lease, none of Cipher's contracted revenue rides a conditional classification through a state audit **[P]**.
*Counterweight:* **all the paper matures 2030–2031** against leases running to 2041+, while Hut 8 funds identical assets with **2042 investment-grade paper at the same coupon**. And the ERCOT insulation cuts the other way on growth: **66% of the 3.2 GW classified sits in the Studied Load tier** — the worst mix in the cohort — so the pipeline that justifies the multiple is the part not yet allocated.

**Reason 3 — the credit wrappers and the tenant base are expanding, not retreating.** Fluidstack has added capacity twice, installed racks under beneficial use, closed ~$1.5B at ~$18B in September, and brought a successor tenant for years 11–20 **[P/T]**.
*Counterweight:* **the wrapper is not what it appears.** EX-10.1 makes the Google support a **capped, declining (redacted-schedule) termination fee paid into a trustee-controlled account, which converts into Google equity in Cipher Barber Lake LLC** and **terminates on expiry of the Fluidstack lease** — so it protects lenders, dilutes Cipher when it fires, and does not reach the ~$5.2B of years 11–20 owed by an **unnamed** counterparty with no disclosed credit support. This ties directly to the §8 pre-mortem.

**Reason 4 — management's operational incentives are correctly set.** The 2026 Key Service Provider PSUs vest on "data center delivery, timely lease payments, interconnection progress, execution of additional HPC leases, securing additional megawatts" — exactly the right metrics for this business **[P]**.
*Counterweight:* the 2021 PSUs vested on a **$10 billion market-cap milestone** — the wrong metric for a company whose problem is per-share value — and the disclosure pattern runs consistently toward disclosing less on items that cut against the headline: Item 8.01 with no exhibit on the largest lease restructuring, a nine-month-stale warrant footnote, no press release on Batch Zero, no customer concentration, no Barber Lake budget.

**What has to be true.** Per-hall delivery lands through Q4 2026–Q1 2027 **without rent credits**; capex peaks and OCF inflects before the 2031 wall matters; ERCOT's spring 2027 allocation preserves the Studied Load pipeline; **and** the market resumes paying for contracted backlog. Those are four independent conditions, the price already assumes all four, and the cohort has demonstrably stopped doing the fourth.

## 18. Final thoughts & conclusion

Pulling every thread together: **Cipher Digital has done the operationally hardest thing in its sector and is being valued as though the hard part is over.** It delivered a hyperscale data hall early, collected the cohort's first HPC rent, holds the best tenant guarantee in the group, insulated its contracted book from the ERCOT process now hanging over its closest peer, and pre-funded its build campus by campus at falling coupons. That is a real franchise and a genuine execution record.

And yet the equity is a **$562.1M sliver of book value under $6.0155B of gross debt**, eroding ~$150M a quarter, with **49.3% of the share count invisible to every screen**; the credit wrapper everyone pays for is a **capped, declining, redacted termination fee payable to the noteholders that converts into Google equity in Cipher's own project subsidiary**; roughly **32% of the newly headlined backlog is owed by an unnamed counterparty with no credit support, starting in 2037**; the cheapest rent in the cohort was traded for that credit; **$359.3M of first-loss construction risk** was traded for duration on megawatts already leased; **66% of the growth pipeline sits in the tier ERCOT has not allocated**; and the whole capital structure must be refinanced in 2031, high-yield, against a peer holding 2042 investment-grade paper at the same coupon. On the diluted count it is the **most expensive name in its cohort per contracted megawatt**.

### Edge — what do you believe that the crowd doesn't?

**The variant view is that this is not a backlog story with timing risk — it is a *capital-structure* story in which the contracted book is real, the equity's claim on it is much smaller than the screens show, and the credit wrapper is structurally aligned against the equity.** Three specifics, none of which is consensus and each of which is verifiable in a filed document:

1. **The denominator.** Fully diluted shares are **619,733,668, +49.3% over the 415,030,722 every aggregator and every per-MW comparison uses** — note-conversion dilution alone is 161,930,746, up 3.2x in a year, with both series in the money at $4.4460 and $16.0325. Because the company loses money in every period, **basic has always equalled diluted and this dilution has never touched reported EPS.** Correct for it and Cipher moves from *mid-range* ($14.9M/MW) to **the most expensive name in its cohort ($22.3M/MW vs Hut 8's $12.6M)** and above UBS's published $12–14M/MW band.
2. **The wrapper protects the lenders and dilutes the equity.** Not "the guarantee vests late" — the guarantee, per EX-10.1, is a **capped and declining FS Termination Fee on a redacted schedule**, paid **into a trustee-controlled account**, on payment of which **"Google will be provided an equity interest position in Landlord"**; Google's liability "IS LIMITED TO THE AMOUNT OF THE FS TERMINATION FEE"; its alternative is to novate in at an explicit **"Discounted Rent"** with an early termination right; and §12.b **terminates the whole arrangement when the Fluidstack lease expires**. The company's own footnote says the **"high-yield bond amortizes in line with the Google backstop."** The wrapper was **sized to the debt** ($1.73B ≈ $1,733M of notes), not to Cipher's equity.
3. **The revenue number itself is negotiable, and one line item will show it.** The Q2 10-Q discloses that Cipher "**may be subject to rent credits or other remedies for failure to achieve specified delivery milestones**" at both Black Pearl and Stingray. **Rent credits are permanent reductions, not deferrals.** The 9/24 amendment arose from "change orders and the continued evolution of tenant requirements" and moved delivery into Q1 2027 — so the conditions for a credit are being created now, and Oracle's force majeure shows the market has begun pricing exactly this class of risk in **credit** (~$18B of Project Jupiter paper at 89–91 cents) before equity notices.

**What this Edge explicitly does not claim.** It does not claim demand fails — hyperscaler capex is up 81%. It does not claim a tenant defaults — Amazon is excellent and Fluidstack just raised $1.5B. It does not claim insolvency — nothing matures before 2030 and $3.73B is escrowed. And it does not claim the sell-side is wrong about *enterprise* value; it claims the sell-side and the market are answering different questions, and that the equity's residual claim is thinner and more contested than a $31 target implies.
